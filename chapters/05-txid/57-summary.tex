\section{Summary}\label{sec:txid-summary}

In this chapter we constructed a tracing protocol in which tags come from a
bounded, epoch-local index space instead of a counter, so users can create
transactions concurrently and in any order. Users grant an auditor permission
to trace them for one epoch, and a permission never exposes users who are not
targeted. We formalised tracing as an ideal functionality, gave a generic
construction, and instantiated it once with algebraic pseudorandom functions
and Sigma protocols, and once with MiMC and a zk-SNARK.

We use an epoch-local index space, giving permissions boundaries in time and
costs that do not grow with the total ledger size.

Several directions remain for future work. Currently, grants must come before
the epoch they cover. Retrospective grants work in the protocol but require a
proof that can explain uniform tags with a key chosen in later epochs. A
threshold auditor, with the techniques of \Cref{chap:tsps}, would split
auditing capabilities between institutions.  Algebraic pseudorandom functions
that compose well with Sigma protocols may require $q$-type assumptions. It is
future work to explore whether the generic construction could be built from a
primitive that avoids this.
