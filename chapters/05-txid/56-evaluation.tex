\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:txid-evaluation}

We evaluate the concrete instantiation of \Cref{sec:txid-instantiation},
benchmarking each relation's proof and verification cost individually before
reporting end-to-end figures for the full protocol. Costs and sizes are reported
separately, since the two are not interchangeable and the second matters more
for a ledger.

\paragraph{Credential proof ($\rel_6'$).} The proof of knowledge of a signature
from the BBS+ signature scheme ranges over the three secrets
$(\sk,\utk',\utk'')$ certified by the credential. The prover computes $E'$, $d$,
and $\bar{E}$, at 3 scalar multiplications in $\group{1}$ given a cached $b$,
and the three first-message elements $t_1$, $t_2$, $t_3$, at one 3-term, one
2-term, and one 3-term multi-exponentiation in $\group{1}$. The verifier
evaluates 2 pairings, on public data, and 3 multi-exponentiations in
$\group{1}$. The proof consists of 3 $\group{1}$ elements $(E',\bar{E},d)$, 3
$\group{1}$ first-message elements, and 8 $\Zp$ responses.

\paragraph{Epoch key proof ($\rel_5$).} The per-epoch part costs the prover 2
exponentiations in $\group{1}$ and 2 $\Zp$ responses, and the verifier 4
exponentiations in $\group{1}$. A user's first grant additionally carries the
group elements $\{W_\gamma\}_{\gamma\in[0,\Max)}$ and the batched proof that
they open to the certified $\utk''$: $\Max$ exponentiations in $\group{2}$ to
form them, and two $\Max$-term multi-exponentiations in $\group{2}$ for
$\widehat{W}$ and $\widehat{V}$ on each side. At $\ell = 10$ over BLS12-381 this
is $\Max \cdot |\group{2}| = 1024 \times 96 = 98$KB of compressed communication,
and it is by some margin the most expensive single operation in the system. The
elements do not depend on the epoch, so this cost is paid once per user and
reused for every later epoch and every auditor. All subsequent grants are
constant size.

\paragraph{Tag proof ($\rel_7'$).} The sigma protocol
of~\Cref{prot:txid-tag-sigma} works entirely in $\group{1}$ and $\group{T}$; no
operation in $\group{2}$ appears, and no cost depends on $\ell$. The prover
computes $U$ and $V$ in $\group{1}$ and $\Xi$ in $\group{T}$, then $A$, $B$, $D$
in $\group{1}$ and $C$, $F$ in $\group{T}$, and $A_\gamma$ in $\group{1}$: in
total 5 exponentiations or small multi-exponentiations in $\group{1}$ and 3 in
$\group{T}$. The verifier evaluates the single pairing $\pairing(V,\ggen_D)$,
together with 4 multi-exponentiations in $\group{1}$ and 3 in $\group{T}$. The
$\group{T}$ operations dominate, which is what the timings below reflect. The
proof carries 2 $\group{1}$ elements ($U$, $V$), 1 $\group{T}$ element ($\Xi$),
the commitment $\com_\gamma$, the six first-message elements, and 6 $\Zp$
responses.

\paragraph{Range proof.} The range check $0 \leq \gamma < \Max$ is where the
dependence on $\ell$ enters. The prover costs $O(\ell)$ exponentiations plus the
Bulletproofs inner product argument of $O(\ell)$ group operations, and the
verifier cost is $O(\ell)$ exponentiations, with a proof of $2\log_2 \ell + 4$
$\group{1}$ elements and 5 $\Zp$ values~\cite{SP:BBBPWM18}. Opening
$\com_\gamma$ to the range argument adds a further 2 exponentiations in
$\group{1}$ on each side and one $\group{1}$ element to the proof.

\paragraph{When each proof is computed.} The proof of $\rel_4$ is computed once
at registration and once per $\IDgrant$ invocation. The $\{W_\gamma\}$
consistency part of $\rel_5$ is computed once per user, and the epoch part once
per epoch. The proofs of $\rel_6'$, $\rel_7'$, and the range argument are
computed per transaction, and are therefore the three that determine throughput.

\paragraph{Transaction and ledger size.} A transaction carries the payload, one
$\group{T}$ tag, two $\group{1}$ commitments, and the three proofs above. The
tag is the largest single component at 384 bytes compressed over BLS12-381, and
it is the component the ledger must retain and index indefinitely, since tracing
is a lookup by tag. Deployments for which this dominates should note that
$\group{T}$ elements admit compression to $\group{T}$'s cyclotomic subgroup,
which we do not apply here.

\paragraph{Cost of tracing.} Tracing one user for one epoch costs $\Max$
pairings and $\Max$ lookups in the tag-indexed ledger, independent of the number
of transactions on that ledger and of the number of registered users.  This is
the comparison that motivates the construction: the revocation-based schemes
of~\Cref{tab:txid-related} require one decryption per ledger entry, so their
cost grows without bound as the system is used, and they expose non-targeted
users to the auditor in the process.

The tag proof, which is the component this chapter contributes, generates in a
few milliseconds and verifies in under 10ms even without optimisation. The
per-transaction total additionally includes the credential proof and the range
argument, neither of which is specific to tracing: the first is a standard BBS+
presentation and the second a standard Bulletproofs range argument, and both
would be present in any anonymous-credential payment system of this shape. The
right way to read the table is therefore as the cost of adding tracing on top of
a credential presentation the system was going to perform anyway.

That framing is also what makes a comparison with Idemix meaningful. Idemix, a
widely deployed anonymous credential system over BLS12-381, requires
approximately 4ms for signing and 3ms for verification with no tracing
support\footnote{Implementation found at \url{https://github.com/ibm/idemix},
commit \textsc{[commit]}.}. Those figures are credential-presentation figures,
so the like-for-like comparison is against our credential proof row, and the tag
and range rows are the marginal cost of tracing. Comparing our epoch-plus-tag
subtotal against Idemix's presentation cost, as an earlier reading of this table
would invite, would compare two different things.
