\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:txid-evaluation}

We evaluate both instantiations of \Cref{sec:txid-instantiation}, benchmarking
each relation's proof and verification cost individually before comparing the
whole against a deployed anonymous credential system. Costs and sizes are
reported separately, since the two are not interchangeable and the second
matters more for a ledger.

\paragraph{Experimental setup.} We implement both instantiations in Go using the
\texttt{gnark-crypto} library~\cite{gnark-crypto}, which provides optimised
multi-pairing routines and assembly level curve arithmetic for both curves we
report. All experiments were run on an Apple M2 laptop with 16GB of RAM, running
four performance and four efficiency cores. Measurements were collected using
Go's built-in benchmarking framework, averaged over the number of iterations
taken to reach one second of computation, and summarised across ten repetitions
with \texttt{benchstat}. Every reported figure carries a variation of at most
4\% across repetitions, and every operation is single-threaded except where
noted.

As a scale for the figures that follow, on this machine one exponentiation in
$\group{1}$ costs $62.6\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ over BN254 and $113.8\,\mu\mathrm{s}$
over BLS12-381, and one pairing evaluation costs 0.956ms and 1.141ms
respectively.

\paragraph{Scope of the measurements.} The prototype implements all four
relations in both instantiations, and every reported figure is a complete cost
for the operation named. The range check on the transaction index is handled
differently by the two, and this is the one place where care is needed in
reading the tables. In the hash based instantiation it is compiled into the
$\rel_7$ circuit as a bit decomposition, and is therefore already inside the
$\rel_7$ rows of \Cref{tab:txid-snark}. In the algebraic instantiation it is a
separate Bulletproofs argument over $\com_\gamma$, reported on its own row of
\Cref{tab:algebraic}, and the per-transaction cost of $\rel_7'$ is the sum of
the sigma protocol and that argument. The total rows of \Cref{tab:algebraic} are
measured as a single end-to-end benchmark rather than summed, so they differ
slightly from the sum of the two component rows.

\paragraph{Choice of $\ell$.} We run the benchmarks with $\ell = 8$, so a user
may submit up to $\Max = 256$ transactions in any one epoch. A user who reaches
that cap waits for the next epoch boundary. An epoch spans $\lambda$ blocks and
$\lambda$ is a deployment parameter, so the cap is really set by choosing
$\lambda$ against the throughput a single user is expected to need. The
choice of $\ell$ trades that cap against three costs that all grow with $\Max$:
the size of $\{W_\gamma\}$, the work an auditor does to trace one epoch, and the
range argument. A power of two is the natural choice for the third of these, for
the reason given below.

\subsection{The hash based instantiation}

For the hash based instantiation of~\Cref{sec:txid-hash-instantiation},
correctness of the two pseudorandom function evaluations is proved with
Groth16~\cite{EC:Groth16}, and each proof is three group elements. $\rel_5$
proves the derivation of the epoch key, and $\rel_7$ additionally proves tag
correctness and that the transaction index lies in range, so the two are
compiled and benchmarked as separate circuits. Verification of either requires 3
pairing evaluations and one multi-exponentiation over the public inputs.
\Cref{tab:txid-snark} reports the results.

The $\rel_7$ circuit is two MiMC permutations together with an $\ell$-bit
decomposition of the transaction index, which is what separates its constraint
count from that of $\rel_5$. At $\ell = 8$ the range check is eight constraints
against the 661 and 667 that a single permutation costs over BN254 and
BLS12-381, so it is free relative to the hashing, and the timings do not move
measurably with $\ell$.

Proof size is the striking figure: 192 bytes per transaction over BLS12-381,
constant in $\ell$ and in the size of the circuit. Verification is also fast, at
around 3ms. The prover pays for both, at 32.3ms per transaction, and the setup
rows are the trusted setup that \Cref{sec:txid-hash-instantiation} gives up
transparency to obtain.

\subsection{The algebraic instantiation}

\paragraph{Registration and grant proof ($\rel_4$).} The proof of knowledge of
the openings of $\PSK$ and $\PTK$ costs the prover 1 exponentiation and 1
two-base multi-exponentiation in $\group{1}$, and 3 $\Zp$ responses. The
verifier costs are 3 exponentiations and 1 two-base multi-exponentiation in
$\group{1}$. The same proof is produced at registration, on the registration
authority's nonce, and at each grant, on $\algoproof_5 \| \rand$. As the
credential signature is sent in the clear at a grant, the auditor additionally
verifies it directly, at the cost of 2 pairing evaluations.

\paragraph{Credential proof ($\rel_6'$).} The proof of knowledge of a signature
from the BBS+ signature scheme ranges over the three secrets
$(\sk,\utk',\utk'')$ certified by the credential. The prover computes 3 scalar
multiplications in $\group{1}$ to randomise the signature, then 3
multi-exponentiations in $\group{1}$, of sizes three, two and three, and sends 8
$\Zp$ responses. The verifier evaluates 2 pairings, on public data, and 3
multi-exponentiations in $\group{1}$. The proof consists of 3 $\group{1}$
elements $(E',\bar{E},d)$, 3 $\group{1}$ first-message elements, and the 8
responses.

\paragraph{Epoch key proof ($\rel_5$).} The per-epoch part costs the prover 2
exponentiations in $\group{1}$ and 2 $\Zp$ responses, and the verifier 4
exponentiations in $\group{1}$. A user's first grant additionally carries the
group elements $\{W_\gamma\}_{\gamma\in[0,\Max)}$ of $\PTK$ and the batched
proof that they are well formed against the certified $\utk''$: $\Max$
exponentiations in $\group{2}$ to form them, and two $\Max$-term
multi-exponentiations in $\group{2}$ for $\widehat{W}$ and $\widehat{V}$ on each
side. This is $\Max \cdot |\group{2}|$ bytes of communication, 24KB at $\ell =
8$ over BLS12-381, and it is the largest single message in the system.

The vector is a public parameter carrying no secret, and it does not vary with
the epoch. How it reaches an auditor is therefore a deployment choice. It may be
sent at that auditor's first grant, or published once alongside $T$ for any
auditor to fetch. Either way it is formed and proven once per user, and
every grant after the first is constant size. Nothing here obliges an auditor to
retain it, which is what distinguishes this cost from persistent per-user state.

\paragraph{Tag proof ($\rel_7'$).} The sigma protocol
of~\Cref{prot:txid-tag-sigma} costs the prover 7 exponentiations in
$\group{1}$, 5 exponentiations in $\group{T}$, and 1 pairing evaluation, and the
verifier 9, 5 and 1 of the same. Opening $\com_\gamma$ to the range argument
accounts for 2 of those $\group{1}$ exponentiations on each side, and for one
$\group{1}$ element of the proof.

Only one pairing is needed on either side. Because $\Pi = \pairing(\ek^{\,s},
\ggen_D) = \pairing(U, \ggen_D)^{\utk'}$, the randomised epoch key is never
formed, and both parties exponentiate $\pairing(U, \ggen_D)$ rather than pairing
a second time. Exponentiation in $\group{T}$ is then the dominant cost, at
0.83ms over BN254 and 1.00ms over BLS12-381, which is close to the cost of a
pairing. The five of them account for roughly three quarters of the sigma
protocol figures in \Cref{tab:algebraic}. The proof carries 1 $\group{1}$
element ($U$), 2 $\group{T}$ elements ($\Pi$, $X$), the commitment
$\com_\gamma$, the six first-message elements, and 6 $\Zp$ responses. The
randomised epoch key $\ek^{\,s}$ is not transmitted.

\paragraph{Range argument.} The range check $0 \leq \gamma < \Max$ is a
Bulletproof over $\com_\gamma$, sharing the bases $\gen_3$ and $\gen_4$ with
that commitment so that the two arguments speak about one $\gamma$. Its inner
product argument halves its vectors each round, so it runs over a vector of
length $\ell$ directly whenever $\ell$ is a power of two, which is why we take
$\ell = 8$. For other $\ell$ the vectors must be padded to $2^{\lceil \log_2
\ell \rceil}$, and the cost is that of the padded length rather than of $\ell$.
So $\ell = 10$ and $\ell = 16$ cost the same. The proof is $2\log_2 \ell + 4$
elements of $\group{1}$ and 5 scalars~\cite{SP:BBBPWM18}, which is 10 group
elements and 5 scalars at $\ell = 8$, or 640 bytes over BLS12-381.

The prover's dominant group operations are the folding of the two generator
vectors, at $2(\ell-1)$ exponentiations in $\group{1}$, together with a further
$\ell-1$ to form the shifted generators and multi-exponentiations of total
length $O(\ell)$ across the $\log_2 \ell$ rounds. The verifier folds the shift
into its exponents and checks the argument as a single multi-exponentiation of
$2\ell + 2\log_2 \ell + 4$ terms, which is 26 terms at $\ell = 8$.

\paragraph{When each proof is computed.} The proof of $\rel_4$ is computed once
at registration and once per $\IDgrant$ invocation. The $\{W_\gamma\}$
consistency part of $\rel_5$ is computed once per user, and the epoch part once
per epoch. The proofs of $\rel_6'$, $\rel_7'$, and the range argument are
computed per transaction, and are therefore the three that determine throughput.

\paragraph{Transaction and ledger size.} A transaction carries the payload, one
$\group{T}$ tag, two $\group{1}$ commitments, and the three proofs above. The
tag is the largest single component at 384 bytes compressed over BLS12-381, and
it is the component the ledger must retain and index indefinitely, since tracing
is a lookup by tag. Deployments for which this dominates should note that
$\group{T}$ elements admit compression to $\group{T}$'s cyclotomic subgroup,
which we do not apply here.

\paragraph{Cost of tracing.} Tracing one user for one epoch costs $\Max$
pairings and $\Max$ lookups in the tag-indexed ledger, independent of the number
of transactions on that ledger and of the number of registered users. The
auditor produces no proof of its own. The $\Max$ pairing evaluations are
mutually independent, so the work parallelises directly, and we report both a
single-threaded figure and one across all eight cores.

\subsection{Comparison with an anonymous credential system}

To place these figures in context we benchmark Hyperledger Idemix on the same
machine and the same curve. Idemix is a deployed anonymous credential system,
and its Aries variant uses BBS+ over BLS12-381, so it is close in structure to
the credential layer of \Cref{sec:txid-instantiation}. We measure it in two
configurations: a standard signature, which offers no oversight mechanism at
all, and one carrying pseudonymous commitments to the enrollment id and
revocation handle, which is Idemix's auditability feature. A fresh pseudonym is
derived per signature in both cases, as unlinkability requires.
\Cref{tab:txid-idemix} reports the full per-transaction cost of each system.

Both of our rows are the sum of a credential proof and a tag proof, and both
include the range check on the transaction index. The hash based row combines
the Groth16 figures of \Cref{tab:txid-snark} with the measured $\rel_6'$ sigma
protocol. That instantiation certifies $(\sk,\utk)$ rather than
$(\sk,\utk',\utk'')$, so its credential proof is in fact marginally cheaper
than the figure used here.

Neither instantiation is uniformly cheaper than Idemix, and neither needs to be.
Idemix offers no efficient tracing at all, and the configuration it does offer
answers a different and weaker question, which the next paragraph makes precise.
The hash based instantiation verifies faster than Idemix does \emph{with} its
audit pseudonyms enabled, at the cost of a substantially slower prover. The
algebraic instantiation proves in about 1.9 times and verifies in about 1.6
times Idemix's cost with those pseudonyms enabled, and requires no trusted setup
anywhere. Since a transaction is generated once and verified by every validator,
it is the verification column that governs throughput, and on verification both
instantiations stay within a small factor of a deployed credential system that
provides no tracing at all.

\paragraph{The cost of tracing, compared.} Idemix's audit mechanism answers a
different question from ours. Given a candidate enrollment id and a signature,
it decides whether the two match. Idemix has no notion of an epoch, so
recovering the transactions a single user submitted over some period requires
one such test per ledger entry in that period. We measure that test at 0.42ms.
Tracing a user over one epoch in our scheme is instead a fixed $\Max$ pairing
evaluations, measured at 0.31s for the algebraic instantiation, and does not
depend on how many transactions the ledger holds. The hash based instantiation
replaces those pairings with $\Max$ evaluations of $\HT$ and so is cheaper again
by orders of magnitude, which makes the algebraic figure the conservative one to
quote here. Both figures are single-threaded, and both scans parallelise in the
same way, since every test and every pairing is independent of the others.

The two approaches cost the same at roughly 750 ledger entries per epoch. At
$10^6$ entries the Idemix approach requires around 7 minutes against our 0.31s,
and the ratio between the two grows linearly in the number of entries. This is
the separation of \Cref{tab:txid-related} made concrete: the revocation-based
schemes there require one decryption per ledger entry, so their cost grows
without bound as the system is used, and they expose non-targeted users to the
auditor in the process. Ours does neither.
