\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:agg-creds-evaluation}

We implement the instantiation of \Cref{sec:agg-creds-instantiation} in Go using
the \texttt{gnark-crypto} library, which provides optimised implementations of
group and pairing operations for the BLS12-381 pairing groups~\cite{gnark-crypto}.
We use version 0.21.0 of the library, and implement both $\AAC$ and $\ATS$.
The baseline is Idemix, which is built on
BBS+~\cite{CCS:CamVan02,SCN:AuSusMu06,CDL16}. We run the upstream
Idemix implementation on the same version of \texttt{gnark-crypto} and on the
same machine as our scheme, so that the comparison is between the schemes and
not their libraries. Idemix issues one credential on all $n$ attributes
from one issuer, and presents it with $k$ attributes disclosed. We take all measurements
on a MacBook Air with an Apple M2 and 16\,GB of RAM, using all eight
cores. Each time is the median of 100 repetitions.

Registration and issuance in \Cref{tab:agg-creds-times} depend on neither $n$
nor $k$. In registration, the user sends 128 bytes and the registration authority
352 bytes, in three messages in total. In issuance, the user sends 440 bytes and
the issuer 224 bytes, in four messages in total.

\Cref{fig:agg-creds-idemix} compares our scheme with Idemix. Idemix proves
knowledge of a signature on all $n$ attributes, so its cost grows with the
attributes held, while ours grows with the attributes disclosed. Idemix proves
knowledge of the $n - k$ hidden attributes, so at fixed $n$ its presentation
cost falls as $k$ grows. At $n = 128$
and $k = 1$, presentation takes 1.82\,ms against 31.9\,ms for Idemix, and
verification 4.05\,ms against 11.8\,ms. At $n = 128$, Idemix verifies faster
once more than about 20 attributes are disclosed, and presents faster once more
than about 85 are, but it is in the single-issuer setting.

Threshold issuance with silent setup has a constant-size presentation of about
5\,KB on BLS12-381~\cite{EPRINT:GGPR25}. Not counting the disclosed values and
their indices, our presentations are smaller for up to about forty disclosed
attributes.

With $k = 64$ disclosed attributes, every operation of the user takes at most
20.1\,ms, which is low enough for client devices.
