\chapter{Conclusion}\label{chap:conclusion}

This thesis argued that practical privacy comes from modelling the participants
and responsibilities that systems already have, and constraining with
cryptography what each one can learn and do, rather than from removing them.
Each chapter starts from a role that exists in practice: certifiers in
\Cref{chap:tsps}, issuers and registration authorities in
\Cref{chap:agg-creds}, auditors in \Cref{chap:txid}, and all entities in the
transaction system in \Cref{chap:offline-payments}.

In each case institution keeps its function without being trusted with more
than that specific function requires. Threshold certifiers sign committed
values, and no single one of them can certify alone. The registration authority
binds a key to a tag and learns nothing else, while issuers stay independent
and keep no state about users. The auditor of \Cref{chap:txid} computes tags
for one user and one epoch, and learns nothing about anyone else.

The same pattern appears in the capabilities that the constructions grant.
Capabilities are scoped by a given user and period. Practicality was a
constraint throughout. The constructions avoid interaction wherever possible.
Threshold signing moves all interaction to an offline phase, issuers of
aggregate credentials never coordinate, users submit transactions for tracing
concurrently without counters, and offline payments need no network at all.
Every construction is implemented and measured, so these claims come with
concrete costs.

Each chapter gives a generic construction from standard primitives, and then an
instantiation tailored to Sigma protocols and pairing product equations. The
price of that efficiency is often a stronger assumption: the GGM through
$\AGHO$, and $q$-type assumptions for BBS+ and the Dodis-Yampolskiy PRF.
Instantiations under standard assumptions with comparable efficiency remain
open.

Our security proofs are in the standalone model, since extraction rewinds, and
composable guarantees would need different proof techniques. The chapters share
a credential layer: a registration authority with BBS+ or SPS credentials. One
system combining credentials, tracing and offline payments deserves a joint
analysis.
