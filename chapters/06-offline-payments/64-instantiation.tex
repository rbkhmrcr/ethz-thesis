\section{Instantiation}\label{sec:offline-instantiation}

\begin{outline}
  \item instantiate $\OP$: every statement proven is an equation between group elements, so every proof is a Sigma protocol
  \item pseudonyms: Pedersen commitments (perfectly hiding, binding under DL, group elements so Schnorr proofs apply)
  \item audit ciphertexts: ElGamal in $\group{1}$, IND-CPA under DDH; decryption gives $\gen^{\algo{uid}}$, auditor looks the identifier up
  \item credentials: BBS+ as in \Cref{chap:txid}, so the two chapters share a credential layer; EUF-CMA; provable via a few discrete-logarithm equations, in a proof that splits so the secure element only touches $\sk$
  \item central bank's SCI: Pointcheval-Sanders with signing on committed inputs; short, rerandomisable signatures, so the proof of withdrawal is a few group elements
  \item all Sigma protocols via Fiat-Shamir, except the withdrawal request proof (Fischlin), the only place online extraction is needed
\end{outline}

Throughout, we use hash functions $\hash_p$ into $\Zp$ and
$\hash_{\group{1}}$ into $\group{1}$. The commitment scheme is the Pedersen
commitment scheme with $n = 1$, with key $\ck = (\hgen_1, \hgen) \leftarrow
\ComGen(\secparam, 1)$, so that a pseudonym is $\com = \hgen_1^{\algo{uid}}
\hgen^{s}$. The BBS+ generators $\gen_0, \ldots, \gen_{\ell+1}$ and the
generators $P_0, \ldots, P_\ell$ of the central bank's signing on committed
inputs are separate from $\ck$. Every generator is obtained as
$\hash_{\group{1}}$ of its own fixed string, so that no participant knows the
discrete logarithm relations among them. For $\msg = (\msg_0, \ldots,
\msg_{\ell-1}) \in \Zp^\ell$ we write $\vec{P}^{\msg\|\openinfo} =
P_\ell^{\openinfo} \prod_{i=0}^{\ell-1} P_i^{\msg_i}$.

\subsection{Instantiating $\JPOK$ with BBS+}\label{sec:joint-proof}

A user's credential is a BBS+ signature $\varphi = (e, r, E)$ from the
registration authority on the pair $(\algo{uid}, \sk)$, with $E = (\gen_0
\gen_1^{\algo{uid}} \gen_2^{\sk} \gen_3^{r})^{1/(z+e)}$, where $z$ is the
registration authority's secret key and $\ggen_Z = \ggen^{z}$ its public key.
Every payment and deposit proves knowledge of such a credential, together with
the opening of the payer's pseudonym, without revealing either.

\begin{outline}
  \item a BBS+ signature cannot be shown directly: verification involves the signed messages, one of which is the identifier
  \item Tessaro-Zhu rerandomisation \cite{EC:TesZhu23b}: $E$ with two fresh exponents becomes $u, w, x$
  \item verification becomes one pairing equation on public values, $\pairing(u, \ggen_Z)\pairing(x, \ggen) = 1$, plus two discrete-logarithm equations proven by a Sigma protocol
  \item the check $u \neq 1$ rules out the trivial solution of the pairing equation
\end{outline}

\begin{outline}
  \item the secure element holds only $\sk$ and cannot produce the proof alone; split between two provers following \cite{USENIX:HesSinSor23}
  \item device proves everything else about the credential with $D = \gen_2^{\sk}\gen_3^{t}$ in place of $\gen_2^{\sk}$
  \item $t$ is disclosed, so the device learns $\gen_2^{\sk}$; $\sk$ protected only by DL hardness, which is what the JPOK definition asks for
  \item difference from the original: there $t$ is derived from a PRF key shared between the provers so they never communicate (and $D$ is rerandomised per presentation); ours are paired at registration and talk directly, so the secure element samples $t$ fresh and sends it (old text had this in more detail)
\end{outline}

Note that all the device receives from the secure element is the commitment
$D = \gen_2^{\sk}\gen_3^{t}$, its randomness $t$, and a proof of knowledge of the
opening, which gives the witness hiding property required of a JPOK: the more
powerful prover never sees $\sk$.

\begin{outline}
  \item device proves two further equations under the same challenge
  \item opening of the payer's pseudonym: shares its response for $\algo{uid}$ with the credential equation; this shared response ties pseudonym to credential
  \item audit ciphertext must encrypt the identifier in the pseudonym; proving it alongside with the same $\algo{uid}$ response opens the pseudonym once per hop instead of twice
  \item the secure element's challenge hashes the signed message, so the one thing it must see is the payment's destination
\end{outline}

\Cref{prim:bbs-joint-proof} gives the proof. It combines the rerandomisation of
Tessaro and Zhu with the split of Hesse, Singh and
Sorniotti~\cite{EC:TesZhu23b,USENIX:HesSinSor23}.

\begin{primitivebox}{The joint proof of knowledge of a BBS+ signature}{bbs-joint-proof}

  Let $\pk_\BBS = (\gen_0, \gen_1, \gen_2, \gen_3, \ggen, \ggen_Z)$, $\ck =
(\hgen_1, \hgen)$ and $\pk_\auditor = \gen^{\mu_\auditor}$. The statement is
$\inst = (\pk_\BBS, \ck, \pk_\auditor, \com, \ciph, m)$, with pseudonym $\com$,
audit ciphertext $\ciph = (c_1, c_2)$ and signed message $m$.

  \begin{description}

    \item[$\Phi \leftarrow \JPOKprove_0(\sk, m) \leftrightarrow
\JPOKprove_1(\algo{uid}, \varphi, s, \rho, m)$ :] The secure element runs
$\JPOKprove_0$ and holds $\sk$. The device runs $\JPOKprove_1$ and holds the
credential
$\varphi = (e, r, E)$ on $(\algo{uid}, \sk)$, the opening $(\algo{uid}, s)$ of
$\com = \hgen_1^{\algo{uid}} \hgen^{s}$, and the randomness $\rho$ of $\ciph =
(\gen^{\rho}, \gen^{\algo{uid}} \pk_\auditor^{\rho})$.

      \begin{enumerate}

	\item The secure element samples $t, \gamma_2, \upsilon \sample \Zp$ and
computes
          %
          \begin{gather*}
          %
            D = \gen_2^{\sk} \gen_3^{t}, \quad a_0 = \gen_2^{\gamma_2}
\gen_3^{\upsilon}, \quad \algo{chal}_0 = \hash_p(\gen_2, \gen_3, D, a_0, m), \\
          %
            \pi_2 = \gamma_2 + \algo{chal}_0\, \sk, \quad \zeta = \upsilon +
\algo{chal}_0\, t.
          %
          \end{gather*}
          %
          It sends $(D, t, a_0, \pi_2, \zeta)$ to the device.

	\item The device samples $\iota, \lambda \sample \Zp^*$, sets $r' = r
- t$, and computes
          %
          \begin{equation*}
          %
            u = E^{\iota/\lambda}, \quad w = (\gen_0 \gen_1^{\algo{uid}} D
\gen_3^{r'})^{-1/\lambda}, \quad x = u^{e} w^{\iota}.
          %
          \end{equation*}

	\item The device samples $\alpha, \beta, \delta, \gamma_1, \gamma_3,
\gamma_s, \gamma_\rho \sample \Zp$ and computes
          %
          \begin{gather*}
          %
            a = u^{\alpha} w^{\beta}, \quad b = w^{\delta} \gen_1^{\gamma_1}
\gen_3^{\gamma_3}, \quad d = \hgen_1^{\gamma_1} \hgen^{\gamma_s}, \quad a_1 =
\gen^{\gamma_\rho}, \quad a_2 = \gen^{\gamma_1} \pk_\auditor^{\gamma_\rho}, \\
          %
            \algo{chal}_1 = \hash_p(\inst, u, w, x, D, a_0, a, b, d, a_1, a_2),
\\
          %
            \eta = \alpha + \algo{chal}_1 e, \quad \nu = \beta + \algo{chal}_1
\iota, \quad \xi = \delta + \algo{chal}_1 \lambda, \\
          %
            \pi_1 = \gamma_1 + \algo{chal}_1 \algo{uid}, \quad \pi_3 =
\gamma_3 + \algo{chal}_1 r', \\
          %
            \pi_s = \gamma_s + \algo{chal}_1 s, \quad
\pi_\rho = \gamma_\rho + \algo{chal}_1 \rho.
          %
          \end{gather*}

	\item The device outputs $\Phi = (u,\allowbreak w,\allowbreak x,\allowbreak D,\allowbreak a_0,\allowbreak \pi_2,\allowbreak \zeta,\allowbreak a,\allowbreak b,
d,\allowbreak a_1,\allowbreak a_2,\allowbreak \eta,\allowbreak \nu,\allowbreak \xi,\allowbreak \pi_1,\allowbreak \pi_3,\allowbreak \pi_s,\allowbreak \pi_\rho)$.

      \end{enumerate}

    \item[$\{0,1\} \leftarrow \JPOKverify(\inst, \Phi)$ :] Recompute
$\algo{chal}_0$ and $\algo{chal}_1$ as above, and output $1$ if and only if all
of the following hold:
      %
      \begin{gather*}
      %
        u \neq 1, \quad \pairing(u, \ggen_Z) \pairing(x, \ggen) = 1, \quad a_0
D^{\algo{chal}_0} = \gen_2^{\pi_2} \gen_3^{\zeta}, \\
      %
        a\, x^{\algo{chal}_1} = u^{\eta} w^{\nu}, \quad b\, (D
\gen_0)^{-\algo{chal}_1} = w^{\xi} \gen_1^{\pi_1} \gen_3^{\pi_3}, \quad d\,
\com^{\algo{chal}_1} = \hgen_1^{\pi_1} \hgen^{\pi_s}, \\
      %
        a_1 c_1^{\algo{chal}_1} = \gen^{\pi_\rho}, \quad a_2
c_2^{\algo{chal}_1} = \gen^{\pi_1} \pk_\auditor^{\pi_\rho}.
      %
      \end{gather*}

  \end{description}

  Without auditing, $\ciph$, $a_1$, $a_2$ and $\pi_\rho$ and the last two
checks are omitted.

\end{primitivebox}

In $\OP$, the signed message is $m = (v, \com_i, \com_{i+1})$ for a payment and
$m = (v, \com_n, \algo{dep})$ for a deposit. The audit proof $\Gamma_i$ of the
construction is the part of $\Phi$ that concerns $\ciph$.

\begin{outline}
  \item pairing check plus the equations on $a$ and $b$ hold exactly when the device knows a valid BBS+ signature on its identifier and the key committed in $D$ \cite{EC:TesZhu23b}
\end{outline}

\subsection{Instantiating $\SCI$ with Pointcheval-Sanders}\label{sec:offline-token-issuance}

We use the Pointcheval-Sanders signature scheme, which also signs vectors of
messages, extended to sign committed inputs as Sanders et al.\ describe it. The
algorithms are given in
\Cref{prim:ps}~\cite{RSA:PoiSan16,RSA:PoiSan18,NDSS:SABMD19}.

Key generation and verification are those of the signature scheme. We abbreviate
Pointcheval-Sanders as PS, index its messages from $0$, and write $[\ell]_0 =
\{0, \ldots, \ell-1\}$.

To request a signature, the recipient commits to the messages, encrypts each
hidden message in the exponent of $k = \hash_{\group{1}}(C)$ under a fresh
ElGamal key of its own, and proves that the commitment and the ciphertexts are
consistent. The signer signs homomorphically on the ciphertexts, and the
recipient decrypts. Disclosed messages are sent in the clear. In $\OP$ the
disclosed message is the token value.

\begin{outline}
  \item why $k$ is a hash of the commitment rather than sampled: both parties agree on it without the signer seeing the messages
  \item recipient key fresh per execution, so two requests cannot be linked by it
  \item $\Pi$ uses Fischlin, not Fiat-Shamir, because online request extractability needs a non-rewinding extractor \cite{C:Fischlin05}; costs a larger proof, computed once per withdrawal by the device
\end{outline}

\begin{primitivebox}{The Pointcheval-Sanders signature scheme}{ps}

  \begin{description}

    \item[$(\sk, \pk) \leftarrow \PSgen(\secparam, \ell)$ :] Sample $\chi,
\theta_0, \ldots, \theta_\ell \sample \Zp$, and output $\sk = (\chi, \theta_0,
\ldots, \theta_\ell)$ and $\pk = (\tilde{x}, \tilde{y}_0, \ldots,
\tilde{y}_{\ell-1}, \tilde{z}, \vec{P})$, where $\tilde{x} = \ggen^{\chi}$,
$\tilde{y}_i = \ggen^{\theta_i}$, $\tilde{z} = \ggen^{\theta_\ell}$, and $\vec{P}
= (P_0, \ldots, P_\ell)$ are the generators for committed inputs.

    \item[$\psi \leftarrow \PSsign(\sk, \msg)$ :] On input $\msg \in
\Zp^{\ell}$, sample $k \sample \group{1}$ and $\omega \sample \Zp$, and output
$\psi = (\omega, k, l)$ with $l = k^{\chi + \sum_{i \in [\ell]_0} \theta_i
\msg_i + \theta_\ell \omega}$.

    \item[$\{0,1\} \leftarrow \PSverify(\pk, \msg, \psi)$ :] On input $\psi =
(\omega, k, l)$, output $1$ if and only if $k \neq 1$ and $\pairing(k, \tilde{x}
\prod_{i \in [\ell]_0} \tilde{y}_i^{\msg_i} \tilde{z}^{\omega}) = \pairing(l,
\ggen)$.

    \item[$(\algo{req}, \state) \sample \SCIrequest(\pk, \msg, \algo{Disc})$ :]
On input $\msg \in \Zp^{\ell}$ and a set $\algo{Disc} \subseteq [\ell]_0$ of
indices to disclose, let $H = [\ell]_0 \setminus \algo{Disc}$. Sample $\openinfo,
\mu \sample \Zp$, set $B = \gen^{\mu}$, and compute
          %
          \begin{equation*}
          %
            C = P_\ell^{\openinfo} \prod_{i \in H} P_i^{\msg_i}, \quad k =
\hash_{\group{1}}(C), \quad \hat{\ciph}_i = (\gen^{\rho_i}, k^{\msg_i}
B^{\rho_i}) \ \text{for } i \in H,
          %
          \end{equation*}
          %
with $\rho_i \sample \Zp$. Compute $\Pi$, a proof with the Fischlin transform
that $C = P_\ell^{\openinfo} \prod_{i \in H} P_i^{\msg_i}$ and $\hat{\ciph}_i =
(\gen^{\rho_i}, k^{\msg_i} B^{\rho_i})$ for every $i \in H$, on witness
$(\openinfo, (\msg_i, \rho_i)_{i \in H})$ and statement $(\algo{Disc}, \vec{P},
\gen, B, C, k, (\hat{\ciph}_i)_{i \in H})$. Output $\algo{req} = (C, B,
(\msg_i)_{i \in \algo{Disc}}, (\hat{\ciph}_i)_{i \in H}, \Pi)$ and $\state =
(\msg, \mu, k)$.

    \item[$\algo{resp} \sample \SCIissue(\sk, \algo{req})$ :] Recompute $k =
\hash_{\group{1}}(C)$, and output $\perp$ if $\Pi$ does not verify. Otherwise
sample $\omega \sample \Zp$, parse $\hat{\ciph}_i = (f_{i,1}, f_{i,2})$, and
output $\algo{resp} = (\omega, f_1, f_2)$ with
          %
          \begin{equation*}
          %
            f_1 = \prod_{i \in H} f_{i,1}^{\theta_i}, \quad f_2 = k^{\chi +
\theta_\ell \omega + \sum_{i \in \algo{Disc}} \theta_i \msg_i} \prod_{i \in H}
f_{i,2}^{\theta_i}.
          %
          \end{equation*}

    \item[$\psi \leftarrow \SCIunblind(\state, \algo{resp})$ :] Compute $l = f_2
/ f_1^{\mu}$, and output $\psi = (\omega, k, l)$ if $\PSverify(\pk, \msg, \psi)
= 1$, and $\perp$ otherwise.

  \end{description}

\end{primitivebox}

The Pointcheval-Sanders signature scheme is existentially unforgeable under the
$q$-modified strong Diffie-Hellman assumption, which \Cref{asm:qmsdh} states as
Pointcheval and Sanders give it~\cite{RSA:PoiSan18}.

\begin{assumptionbox}{$q$-Modified Strong Diffie-Hellman ($q$-MSDH)}{qmsdh}

  The $q$-MSDH assumption holds if, given $(\gen^{\chi^i},
\ggen^{\chi^i})_{i=0}^{q}$ and $(\gen^{\varsigma}, \ggen^{\varsigma},
\ggen^{\chi\varsigma})$ for uniformly random $\chi, \varsigma \sample \Zp^*$, no
PPT adversary can output a tuple $(c, \mathcal{P}, Q, Q^{1/(\chi+c)},
Q^{\varsigma/\mathcal{P}(\chi)})$ with non-negligible probability, where $Q \in
\group{1}$ is not the identity and $\mathcal{P}$ is a polynomial of degree at most
$q$ with $\mathcal{P}(-c) \neq 0$.

\end{assumptionbox}

\begin{outline}
  \item the instantiation has the three SCI properties
  \item one-more unforgeability: $q$-MSDH plus the analysis of Sanders et al.\ \cite{NDSS:SABMD19}
  \item request hiding: commitment, ciphertexts under a fresh key and ZK proof reveal nothing about the hidden messages
  \item online request extractability: Fischlin gives a non-rewinding extractor for $\Pi$
  \item details in the proof of \Cref{thm:offline-instantiation-security}
\end{outline}

This protocol adapts directly to the withdrawal step of \Cref{prot:offline}: we
execute it on the vector of messages $(\algo{uid}_0, v, s_0)$ with randomness
$\openinfo = 0$. We set $\algo{Disc} = \{1\}$, so the central bank sees only the
value.

\begin{outline}
  \item why $\openinfo = 0$: $s_0$ is already uniform and hides the commitment, so $C$ is exactly the request commitment of the construction
\end{outline}

The signature $\psi$ can never be shown to a payee, since verifying it needs
$\algo{uid}_0$. The device instead proves once that it holds a valid signature
on the token value and on the identifier and randomness committed in its
pseudonym. It rerandomises $(k, l)$ to $(k^*, l^*)$ and proves the remaining
equation by a Schnorr proof whose challenge hashes the value, the pseudonym and
$(k^*, l^*)$. The proof of withdrawal $\Sigma$ is the first entry of every
token's history, and every payee verifies it.

\begin{outline}
  \item why the challenge hashes value, pseudonym and $(k^*, l^*)$: none of them can be swapped later
\end{outline}

Since $s_0$ is random and not known, $(k^*, l^*)$ leaks no information about
$\algo{uid}_0$. To prove that $\tau$ was issued by the central bank, the token's
owner uses their mobile device to compute $\Sigma$ as follows.

\begin{protocolbox}{The proof of withdrawal}{ps-proof}

  Let $\pk_{\algo{PS}} = (\tilde{x}, \tilde{y}_0, \tilde{y}_1, \tilde{y}_2,
\tilde{z}, \vec{P})$ be the central bank's public key, and $\tau = (v, \com)$ a
token with $\com = \hgen_1^{\algo{uid}} \hgen^{s}$ and $\psi = (\omega, k, l)$ a
Pointcheval-Sanders signature on $(\algo{uid}, v, s)$.

  \begin{description}

    \item[$\Sigma \leftarrow \algo{ProveWithdrawal}(\pk_{\algo{PS}}, \tau,
\algo{uid}, s, \psi)$ :] Do the following:

      \begin{enumerate}

	\item Sample $\vartheta \sample \Zp^*$ and set $(k^*, l^*) =
(k^{\vartheta}, l^{\vartheta})$.

	\item Compute $\tilde{c} = \tilde{y}_0^{\algo{uid}} \tilde{y}_2^{s}
\tilde{z}^{\omega}$.

	\item Sample $\alpha, \beta, \delta \sample \Zp$ and compute $a =
\hgen_1^{\alpha} \hgen^{\beta}$, $\tilde{a} = \tilde{y}_0^{\alpha}
\tilde{y}_2^{\beta} \tilde{z}^{\delta}$, and
          %
          \begin{equation*}
          %
            \algo{chal} = \hash_p(\pk_{\algo{PS}}, \ck, v, \com, k^*, l^*,
\tilde{c}, a, \tilde{a}).
          %
          \end{equation*}

	\item Compute $\pi_{\algo{uid}} = \alpha + \algo{chal}\, \algo{uid}$,
$\pi_s = \beta + \algo{chal}\, s$ and $\pi_\omega = \delta + \algo{chal}\,
\omega$, and output $\Sigma = (k^*, l^*, \tilde{c}, a, \tilde{a},
\pi_{\algo{uid}}, \pi_s, \pi_\omega)$.

      \end{enumerate}

    \item[$\{0,1\} \leftarrow \algo{VerifyWithdrawal}(\pk_{\algo{PS}}, \tau,
\Sigma)$ :] Recompute $\algo{chal}$ as above, and output $1$ if and only if
      %
      \begin{gather*}
      %
        k^* \neq 1, \quad \pairing(l^*, \ggen) = \pairing(k^*, \tilde{x}\,
\tilde{c}\, \tilde{y}_1^{v}), \\
      %
        a\, \com^{\algo{chal}} = \hgen_1^{\pi_{\algo{uid}}} \hgen^{\pi_s}, \quad
\tilde{a}\, \tilde{c}^{\algo{chal}} = \tilde{y}_0^{\pi_{\algo{uid}}}
\tilde{y}_2^{\pi_s} \tilde{z}^{\pi_\omega}.
      %
      \end{gather*}

  \end{description}

\end{protocolbox}

\subsection{Instantiating $\OP$}\label{sec:offline-full-inst}

\Cref{tab:offline-instantiation} lists the instantiation of each generic
component of $\OP$ and the assumption it rests on. The registration authority
issues credentials with the BBS+ signature scheme, blindly, so that the secret
key never leaves the secure element. The central bank signs tokens on committed
inputs, learning neither the identifier nor the serial number. The audit
ciphertext is an ElGamal encryption of $\gen^{\algo{uid}}$ under the auditor's
key, proven consistent with the payer's pseudonym inside the JPOK.

\begin{table}[htbp] \centering \small \begin{tabular}{l|l|l} \toprule Component &
Instantiation & Assumption \\ \midrule Commitment scheme & Pedersen in
$\group{1}$ & DL in $\group{1}$ \\ Credential signature scheme & BBS+ & $q$-SDH \\
SCI scheme & Pointcheval-Sanders & $q$-MSDH \\ Public key encryption scheme &
ElGamal in $\group{1}$ & DDH in $\group{1}$ \\ JPOK &
BBS+ joint proof & ROM \\ Proof of withdrawal &
Schnorr proof & ROM \\ \bottomrule \end{tabular}
\caption[Instantiation of each component of offline payments]{Instantiation of
each generic component of $\OP$, with the assumption under which it is secure:
the discrete logarithm (DL) assumption, DDH, $q$-SDH, $q$-MSDH, and the random
oracle model (ROM). Every proof is a Sigma protocol, compiled with the
Fiat-Shamir transform or, for the withdrawal request, the Fischlin transform, and
so also relies on the DL assumption in $\group{1}$.}
\label{tab:offline-instantiation} \end{table}

Every proof in the instantiation is a Sigma protocol. All of them except the
proof in the withdrawal request are compiled with the Fiat-Shamir transform, and
are therefore simulation extractable in the random oracle
model~\cite{C:FiaSha86,INDOCRYPT:FKMV12}.

\begin{outline}
  \item includes the JPOK, a conjunction of such protocols, giving simulation extractability rather than only the soundness shown in \cite{USENIX:HesSinSor23}
\end{outline}

The proof in the withdrawal request is compiled with the Fischlin transform, and
is also online extractable, as the construction requires. We obtain the
following theorem.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:offline-instantiation-security}
%
  If the discrete logarithm and DDH assumptions hold in $\group{1}$, the $q$-SDH
assumption holds for the BBS+ signature scheme, and the $q$-MSDH assumption
holds for the Pointcheval-Sanders signature scheme, then the instantiated
offline payment protocol securely realises ideal functionality
$\idfu{\algo{op}}$ in the random oracle model.
%
\end{theorem}
