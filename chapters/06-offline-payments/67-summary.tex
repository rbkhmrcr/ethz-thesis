\section{Summary}\label{sec:offline-summary}

This chapter gives $\OP$, a transferable offline payment scheme for CBDC whose
payments are anonymous and whose withdrawals cannot be linked to deposits. A
payee accepts a payment from the transcript alone and can pay the token onward
without contacting anyone, so the scheme keeps working without a network.

Double spending is both prevented and deterred. A secure element enforces that
each token is used once, and each payment carries an encryption of the payer's
identifier under the auditor's key, so the auditor revokes the anonymity of a
double spender and no one else. Because a secure element cannot run the cryptography alone, it
holds only the secret key and produces its part of a joint proof of knowledge,
and the user's phone does the rest.

We prove that $\OP$ securely realises $\idfu{\algo{op}}$ when its building
blocks have the properties of \Cref{thm:offline-construction-security}, and
that its instantiation does so under the DL, DDH, $q$-SDH and $q$-MSDH
assumptions in the random oracle model. A smart card authorises a payment in
under $400\,\mathrm{ms}$, the payer's phone needs under $50\,\mathrm{ms}$, and a
payee verifies a chain of $32$ hops in under a second, with payments growing by
$912$ bytes per hop.

Divisibility remains future work, and revocation offline is limited to expiry
by epochs. Revoking the anonymity of double spenders efficiently without a
trusted auditor is also future work.
