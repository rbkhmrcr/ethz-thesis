\section{Summary}\label{sec:offline-summary}

We gave a transferable offline payment scheme $\OP$, whose payments are
anonymous and whose withdrawals cannot be linked to deposits. We proved that
$\OP$ securely realises $\idfu{\algo{op}}$ when its building blocks have the
properties of \Cref{thm:offline-construction-security}, and that its
instantiation does so under the DL, DDH, $q$-SDH and $q$-MSDH assumptions in the
random oracle model. A smart card authorises a payment in under
$400\,\mathrm{ms}$, the payer's phone needs under $50\,\mathrm{ms}$, and a payee
verifies a chain of $32$ hops in under a second, with payments growing by $912$
bytes per hop.

Availability does not require removing institutions. The secure element is
issued by an institution and trusted only to keep the user's key. Its
single-use rule makes double spending expensive, but identification does not
rest on it. The phone, which users control, does the heavy computation.
Splitting the joint proof so that the secure element's share costs about as
much as an ordinary signature lets the scheme run on existing hardware. The same
split applies wherever a small trusted component vouches for a statement too
expensive for it to prove, such as hardware wallets, identity cards and device
attestation. A trusted execution environment on the phone could replace the
card if it protected the single-use logic as well as the key.

The auditor's capability is triggered by an event, a double spend, rather than
granted against a particular person, and the role can be distributed by
threshold decryption. \Cref{chap:txid} makes the same move with a different
accountability measure, a grant limited to one user and one epoch. The two
chapters share a credential layer, and a joint analysis is the next step, so
that one registration supports online payments traceable under a grant and
offline payments traced only on double spending.

What remains open is mostly practical. Fixed denominations keep deposits simple,
and aggregating deposits under value commitments would also hide the value at
deposit, at the cost of a more involved deposit proof. Divisibility remains
future work, and revocation offline is limited to expiry by epochs.
