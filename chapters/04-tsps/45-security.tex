\section{Security}\label{sec:tsps-security}

\subsection{Proof of the generic construction}\label{sec:tsps-proof-1}

The construction of \Cref{sec:tsps-construction} is described entirely in terms
of calls to $\algo{MPC}$, over $\Zp$ and over $\group{}$, together with
operations the signers perform locally. We therefore prove security relative to
the properties $\algo{MPC}$ is assumed to satisfy in \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc}, rather
than to any concrete protocol realising it. Existing MPC results provide such
realisations, with security against either an honest or a dishonest majority, as
discussed in \Cref{sec:tsps-evaluation}.

We rely on the following properties:

\begin{enumerate}

  \item[(P1)] The underlying structure preserving signature scheme $\SPS$ is
EUF-CMA secure: no PPT adversary can produce a valid signature on a message
vector it never queried.

  \item[(P2)] The secret sharing underlying $\algo{MPC}$ is a linear $(t+1,
n)$-threshold sharing, so that any $t$ shares are uniform and independent of the
shared value and reconstruction from any $t+1$ shares is a fixed linear map. It
is also private in the sense of \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc}, so that the joint view of
at most $t$ corrupted signers over any sequence of calls is produced by a
simulator $\algo{Sim}$ from their own inputs and outputs together with the
values disclosed by $\algo{Open}$.

  \item[(P3)] The signing algorithm of $\SPS$ admits the decomposition of
\Cref{sec:tsps-construction}, so that the message-dependent part of signing uses
only operations the signers perform locally.

\end{enumerate}

We use two consequences of (P2). First, any $t$ shares of a value may be sampled
uniformly and independently without reference to the value itself.  Second,
given a reconstructed value $w$ together with any $t$ shares of a sharing
$\shr{w}$, every remaining share of $\shr{w}$ is determined and efficiently
computable, since reconstruction is a fixed linear map on any $t+1$ shares. The
second is what allows a reduction that does not know the signing key to
nevertheless produce honest signature shares, once it has obtained the
corresponding signature from its own oracle.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{lem:tsps-construction-correctness}]

  Fix $(\pk, \shr{\sk}) \sample \TSPSgen(\secparam, \ell, t, n)$, a
pre-processed state $\shr{\state} \sample \algo{OffSign}(\shr{\sk})$, a message
$\vec{\msg} \in \group{1}^\ell$, and a set $Q$ of $t+1$ signers.

  By construction, $\TSPSgen$ calls $\algo{Open}$ only on the group elements
comprising $\pk$, and every value it opens is obtained by applying the steps of
$\SPSgen$ to sharings produced by $\algo{RandShare}$ and $\algo{Mul}$.  Writing
$\sk$ for the value reconstructed by the sharing $\shr{\sk}$, the published
$\pk$ is therefore exactly the public key that $\SPSgen$ associates with $\sk$.

  By (P3), the signature is a product of a message-independent part, computed
during $\algo{OffSign}$ and stored in $\shr{\state}$, and a message-dependent
part computed locally during $\algo{OnSign}$ from $\shr{\sk}$, $\shr{\state}$
and $\vec{\msg}$. Every operation in both parts is either a call to
$\algo{MPC}$, which by security with abort returns either the correct output or
$\bot$, or a local operation preserved by linearity. The shares
$\{\SPSsig_i\}_{i \in Q}$ are therefore a $(t+1, n)$ sharing of the value
$\SPSsign(\sk, \vec{\msg}; \varrho)$, where $\varrho$ denotes the randomness
reconstructed by the sharings that $\algo{OffSign}$ sampled. Since $|Q| = t+1$
and reconstruction does not depend on which $t+1$ shares are used,
$\TSPScombine$ returns that value. Correctness of $\SPS$ then gives
$\SPSverify(\pk, \vec{\msg}, \SPSsig) = 1$.

\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tsps-key-secrecy} Let $\algo{MPC}$ satisfy the
properties of \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc}. Then the joint view of any set of at most $t$
signers, over key generation and over any polynomial number of invocations of
$\algo{OffSign}$ and $\algo{OnSign}$, is independent of the signing key $\sk$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}

  The view consists of the corrupted signers' shares, the transcript of the
calls to $\algo{MPC}$, the values disclosed by $\algo{Open}$, and the public
key. By (P2) the shares of at most $t$ signers are uniform and independent of
every value they share, including $\sk$ and each intermediate value derived from
it. The transcript is produced by $\algo{Sim}$ from those shares and the
disclosed values alone. It therefore suffices to check the disclosed values.
$\TSPSgen$ opens only $\pk$. $\algo{OffSign}$ opens only the masks internal to
$\algo{Inv}$ and $\algo{ExpGrp}$, which by \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc} are uniform and
independent of their arguments.  $\algo{OnSign}$ opens nothing.  $\TSPScombine$
opens the signature, which by \Cref{lem:tsps-construction-correctness} is a
signature $\SPS$ itself produces. The view is thus a function of $\pk$, of
signatures under $\pk$, and of values independent of $\sk$, and reveals nothing
further about $\sk$ than an ordinary signing oracle would.

\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tsps-transparency} Let $\algo{MPC}$ satisfy the
properties of \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc}. Then a public key output by $\TSPSgen$ is
distributed identically to one output by $\SPSgen$, and a signature output by
$\TSPScombine$ is distributed identically to one output by $\SPSsign$ under that
key. Verification is syntactically unchanged, $\TSPSverify = \SPSverify$, so a
verifier cannot distinguish a threshold signature from an ordinary one, and
needs to know neither $n$, nor $t$, nor which signers took part.  \end{lemma}

\begin{proof}

  $\TSPSgen$ samples each field element of $\sk$ by a call to
$\algo{RandShare}$, whose reconstruction is uniform on $\Zp$, and derives every
further value by the same equations $\SPSgen$ uses. The reconstructed key is
therefore distributed as $\SPSgen$ would produce, and $\pk$ is the corresponding
public key. Likewise, $\algo{OffSign}$ samples the signing randomness by calls
to $\algo{RandShare}$, so the value $\varrho$ appearing in the proof of
\Cref{lem:tsps-construction-correctness} is distributed as $\SPSsign$ would
sample it, and the combined signature is $\SPSsign(\sk, \vec{\msg}; \varrho)$.
Verification is that of $\SPS$ applied unchanged.

\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security}]

  Let $\adv$ be a PPT adversary against adp-TS-UF-1 security. We build $\advB$
playing the EUF-CMA game of Experiment~\ref{exp:sps-uf-cma} against $\SPS$.

  \paragraph{Setup.} $\advB$ receives $\pk$ from its own challenger and forwards
the setup parameters to $\adv$. On receiving $(n, t, \Cor_0)$ from $\adv$, it
samples a set $\mathcal{K} \subseteq [1,n]$ with $|\mathcal{K}| = t$ uniformly
at random, and aborts if $\Cor_0 \not\subseteq \mathcal{K}$.  $\advB$ then
samples key shares $\{\sk_i\}_{i \in \mathcal{K}}$ uniformly and independently,
and sends $\pk$ and $\{\sk_i\}_{i \in \Cor_0}$ to $\adv$.

  This is the one place the reduction departs from the real game, and it is
worth being explicit about why. $\advB$ does not know $\sk$, so it cannot hold
all $n$ shares: any $t+1$ of them would reconstruct the key its own challenger
is protecting. It holds exactly $t$, which by (P2) it may sample uniformly, and
which are therefore distributed exactly as in a real execution. The set
$\mathcal{K}$ is a guess at which signers $\adv$ will either corrupt, or ask for
a partial signature on its eventual forgery message. The guess is what the
reduction's loss pays for, and it is also what lets the reduction handle
adaptive corruption without equivocating a sharing after the fact.

  \paragraph{Simulating the offline oracle.} On a query
$\mathcal{O}_{\algo{Off}}(\mathsf{sid})$ with $\mathsf{sid}$ fresh, $\advB$
samples the shares $\{\state_{\mathsf{sid},i}\}_{i \in \mathcal{K}}$ uniformly,
which by (P2) is their real distribution, and runs $\algo{Sim}$ on the corrupted
signers' inputs and these outputs to produce the transcript that $\adv$ sees. If
$\adv$, acting for the corrupted signers, causes the execution to abort, $\advB$
returns $\bot$ and marks $\mathsf{sid}$ unusable, exactly as security with abort
permits. An aborted session yields no pre-processed state and so cannot
contribute to a forgery. By (P2) the simulated transcript is distributed
identically to a real one.

  Note that $\advB$ never fixes the value $\varrho$ that $\shr{\state}$ shares.
It fixes only $t$ shares of it, which are independent of $\varrho$, and the
value is determined later, implicitly, by whichever signature $\advB$ obtains
for that session. This is sound precisely because pre-processed state is
single-use. Were one session consumed by two messages, both signatures would
have to be consistent with a single $\varrho$, which $\advB$ cannot arrange,
since its own oracle chooses fresh randomness per query.

  \paragraph{Simulating the partial signing oracle.} On a query $\TSPSsigno(i,
\mathsf{sid}, \vec{\msg})$ with $i \in \mathcal{H}$ and $\mathsf{sid}$
initialised and unused for signing, $\advB$ proceeds in one of two ways.

  If $i \in \mathcal{K}$, then $\advB$ holds $\sk_i$ and
$\state_{\mathsf{sid},i}$, and by (P3) the online phase is a local computation
on exactly these and $\vec{\msg}$. It runs $\algo{OnSign}$ and returns the
result. No query to its own oracle is made.

  If $i \notin \mathcal{K}$, then $\advB$ queries its own signing oracle on
$\vec{\msg}$, obtaining $\SPSsig$, and records $\vec{\msg}$ as queried. It
computes the $t$ shares $\{\SPSsig_j\}_{j \in \mathcal{K}}$ itself by running
$\algo{OnSign}$ on the material it holds, and then, by the second consequence of
(P2), derives the remaining share $\SPSsig_i$ from $\SPSsig$ and those $t$
shares. It caches the derived shares against $\mathsf{sid}$, so that repeated
queries in the same session are answered consistently. The share returned is the
unique one consistent with a genuine execution, so $\adv$'s view is again
identical to the real game.

  \paragraph{Simulating corruption.} On a query $\corrupto(k)$, $\advB$ returns
$\sk_k$ together with every $\state_{\mathsf{sid},k}$ it holds, if $k \in
\mathcal{K}$, and aborts otherwise. It also aborts if $|\Cor|$ would exceed $t$,
as the game requires.

  \paragraph{Analysis.} $\adv$ eventually outputs $(\vec{\msg}^*, \SPSsig^*)$,
which $\advB$ forwards to its own challenger. Suppose $\adv$ wins, so
$\SPSverify(\pk, \vec{\msg}^*, \SPSsig^*) = 1$, $|\Cor| \leq t$, and
$|S(\vec{\msg}^*)| < t + 1 - |\Cor|$, that is $|S(\vec{\msg}^*)| \leq t -
|\Cor|$. Then
  %
  \begin{equation*}
  %
    \left| \Cor \cup S(\vec{\msg}^*) \right| \leq |\Cor| + |S(\vec{\msg}^*)|
\leq t,
  %
  \end{equation*}
  %
  so there is at least one size-$t$ subset of $[1,n]$ containing $\Cor \cup
S(\vec{\msg}^*)$. Conditioned on $\mathcal{K}$ being such a subset, $\advB$
never aborts on a corruption query, and never queries its own signing oracle on
$\vec{\msg}^*$, since every honest signer that issued a partial signature on
$\vec{\msg}^*$ lies in $\mathcal{K}$. Its output therefore satisfies the EUF-CMA
winning condition.

  By (P2), the shares $\advB$ hands out are identically distributed whichever
$\mathcal{K}$ was chosen, so $\mathcal{K}$ is independent of $\adv$'s view and
the conditioning does not change $\adv$'s success probability. Since
$\mathcal{K}$ is uniform among the $\binom{n}{t}$ subsets of size $t$, the event
has probability at least $\binom{n}{t}^{-1}$, and
  %
  \begin{equation*}
  %
    \Adv_{\TSPS, \adv}^{\text{adp-TS-UF-1}}(\secparam) \leq \binom{n}{t} \cdot
\Adv_{\SPS, \advB}^{\text{EUF-CMA}}(\secparam),
  %
  \end{equation*}
  %
  which is negligible by (P1).

  \paragraph{On the loss.} The factor $\binom{n}{t}$ is the price of two things
at once. The first is adaptive corruption, since $\mathcal{K}$ must be fixed
before $\adv$ chooses whom to corrupt. The second is the freedom $\adv$ has in
choosing which honest signers issue partial signatures on its forgery message.
It vanishes in the case that matters most in practice, $|\Cor| = t$ with the
corrupted set fixed in advance: the winning condition then forces
$|S(\vec{\msg}^*)| = 0$, so $\mathcal{K} = \Cor$ is determined and no guess is
needed. Removing the loss in general would require either a sharing that can be
equivocated after the fact, or an assumption of secure erasures, neither of
which the realisations benchmarked in \Cref{sec:tsps-evaluation} provide.

\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of the instantiations}\label{sec:tsps-proof-2}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{thm:tsps-security}]

  It suffices to check that each of the two schemes of
\Cref{sec:tsps-instantiation} satisfies (P1), (P2) and (P3), and to verify the
algebra that \Cref{lem:tsps-construction-correctness} appeals to.

  For (P1), the scheme of \Cref{sec:tsps-sps-ggm} is existentially unforgeable
in the generic group model, and the scheme of \Cref{sec:tsps-sps-kiltz} is
existentially unforgeable under the DDH assumption in both source
groups~\cite{C:AGHO11,C:KilPanWee15}. EUF-CMA is the weaker of the two notions
of \Cref{sec:tsps-definitions}, since it excludes any $\vec{\msg}^*$ the
adversary queried, whereas sUF-CMA also counts a second, different signature on
a queried message as a forgery. Existential unforgeability of either scheme is
therefore exactly what (P1) asks for. As \Cref{sec:tsps-definitions} notes, the
reduction below never needs the stronger notion: it forwards a forgery on a
message it never queried, so requiring sUF-CMA of the underlying scheme would
restrict the constructions the theorem covers without strengthening the
conclusion.

  For (P2), both threshold schemes share their secret key using the linear
$(t+1, n)$ sharing of \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc}, which is linearity preserving and
private against any set of at most $t$ signers by assumption.

  For (P3), the required decomposition is exhibited for each scheme alongside
its description. In \Cref{prot:tsps-abe} the message enters only through the
terms $\msg_i^{-\rho\mu_i}$, and in \Cref{prot:tsps-kiltz} only through $(\gen,
\vec{\msg})^{\mat{K}}$. In both cases a public group element is raised to a
secret-shared field element, which by the linearity preservation of
\Cref{sec:tsps-mpc} each signer computes locally, and the remaining online
operations are products of shares, also local. Everything else in either signing
algorithm depends only on the secret key and on randomness sampled independently
of the message, and so is carried out during $\algo{OffSign}$.

  For correctness, we check in each case that the value reconstructed by
$\TSPScombine$ is the signature the underlying scheme produces under the
reconstructed key and randomness. For \Cref{prot:tsps-abe}, writing $\rho$ for
the value shared by $\shr{\rho}$, the combining party reconstructs
  %
  \begin{equation*}
  %
    \widetilde{r} = \ggen^{1/\rho}, \qquad \widetilde{s} = \ggen^{\tau/\rho} =
\widetilde{r}^{\,\tau}, \qquad z = \gen^{\rho} \prod_{i=1}^{\ell}
\msg_i^{-\rho\mu_i} = \left( \gen \prod_{i=1}^{\ell} \msg_i^{-\mu_i}
\right)^{\rho},
  %
  \end{equation*}
  %
  which is exactly $\SPSsign((\vec{\mu}, \tau), \vec{\msg}; \rho)$ for the
scheme of \Cref{sec:tsps-sps-ggm}. For \Cref{prot:tsps-kiltz}, writing $\rho$
and $\sigma$ for the values shared by $\shr{\rho}$ and $\shr{\sigma}$, the
combining party reconstructs $\widetilde{q} = \ggen^{\sigma}$, $\vec{r} =
\vec{b}^{\,\rho}$, $\vec{s} = \vec{b}^{\,\rho\sigma}$, and
  %
  \begin{equation*}
  %
    \vec{z} = (\gen, \vec{\msg})^{\mat{K}} \cdot \vec{x}^{\,\rho}
\vec{y}^{\,\rho\sigma} = (\gen, \vec{\msg})^{\mat{K}} \cdot \left( \vec{x} \,
\vec{y}^{\,\sigma} \right)^{\rho},
  %
  \end{equation*}
  %
  which is exactly $\SPSsign((\mat{K}, \vec{b}, \vec{x}, \vec{y}), \vec{\msg};
\rho, \sigma)$ for the scheme of \Cref{sec:tsps-sps-kiltz}. Correctness of the
two underlying schemes then gives correctness of the two threshold schemes, and
both fall within the scope of \Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security}, which gives
the claim.

\end{proof}
