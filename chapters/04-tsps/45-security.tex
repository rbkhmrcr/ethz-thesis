\section{Security}\label{sec:tsps-security}

\subsection{Proof
of~\Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security}}\label{sec:tsps-proof-1}

The construction of \Cref{sec:tsps-construction} is described entirely in terms
of calls to $\algo{MPC}$, over $\Zp$ and over $\group{}$, together with
operations the signers perform locally. We therefore prove security relative to
the properties $\algo{MPC}$ is assumed to satisfy in \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc},
rather than to any concrete protocol realising it. Existing MPC results provide
such realisations, with security against either an honest or a dishonest
majority, as discussed in \Cref{sec:tsps-evaluation}.

We rely on the following properties:

\begin{enumerate}

  \item[(P1)] The underlying structure-preserving signature scheme $\SPS$ is
    wUF-CMA secure: no PPT adversary can produce a valid signature on a message
    vector it never queried.

  \item[(P2)] The secret sharing underlying $\algo{MPC}$ is
    linearity-preserving and private, as required in \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc}: any
    set of at most $t$ shares is distributed independently of the shared value.

  \item[(P3)] The signing algorithm of $\SPS$ admits the decomposition of
    \Cref{sec:tsps-construction}, so that the message-dependent part of signing
    uses only operations the signers perform locally.

\end{enumerate}

\begin{proof}

  We argue by contrapositive. Suppose the threshold structure-preserving
  signature scheme does not achieve adaptive TS-UF-1 security. Using the
  adversary $\adv$ of the adp-TS-UF-1 game as a subroutine, we construct an
  adversary $\advB$ that wins the wUF-CMA game of
  Experiment~\ref{exp:sps-uf-cma} against the underlying structure-preserving
  signature scheme, with advantage at least that of $\adv$ in the adp-TS-UF-1
  game.

  $\advB$ forwards the setup parameters it receives to $\adv$. On receiving
  $(n, t, \Cor)$ from $\adv$, and $\pk$ from its own challenger, $\advB$
  samples random shares $\{\sk_i\}_{i \in [1, t-1]}$ and their corresponding
  public key shares, together with shares $\{\sk_i\}_{i \in \Cor}$ and their
  corresponding public key shares, consistent with the sharing $\shr{\pk}$ of
  $\pk$. It forwards $\pk$, $\{\sk_i\}_{i \in \Cor}$, and $\shr{\pk}$ to
  $\adv$.

  \paragraph{Simulating the calls to $\algo{MPC}$.} Because $\advB$ itself
  samples every key share $\{\sk_i\}_{i \in [1,n]}$ — honest shares of its own
  choosing, corrupt shares supplied by $\adv$ — it holds every secret value
  that $\algo{KeyGen}$, $\algo{OffSign}$, and $\algo{OnSign}$ derive and pass
  between calls: by (P3), every intermediate field and group element is a fixed
  function of these key shares and of freshly sampled randomness. This lets
  $\advB$ answer each call $\adv$ makes on behalf of the honest signers exactly
  as \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc} specifies. For a randomised interface
  ($\algo{RandShare}$ and $\algo{RandShareGrp}$), $\advB$ samples the honest
  signers' shares itself, consistently with whatever corrupt shares $\adv$
  supplies, exactly as an honest execution would. For a deterministic one
  ($\algo{Mul}$, $\algo{Inv}$, $\algo{ExpGrp}$, or $\algo{Open}$), $\advB$
  computes the specified output directly from the secret values it already
  holds. Operations the signers perform locally need no simulation at all,
  since $\adv$ computes them on its own shares. In every case the shares
  $\advB$ returns are distributed identically to those a real execution would
  produce, so $\adv$'s view of key generation and of every offline phase is
  perfectly simulated. Note in particular that this leaves $\adv$ no scope to
  win by causing $\algo{MPC}$ to behave incorrectly: $\advB$ does not
  approximate it, it realises it exactly.

  \paragraph{Simulating the online phase and bounding forgeries.} $\advB$
  answers $\adv$'s $\TSPSsigno$ queries in one of two ways. If, for the queried
  $\vec{m}$, $|S(\vec{m})| < t - |\Cor|$, then $\advB$ forms the requested
  signature share $\sigma_i$ directly from its sampled $\sk_i$, using the local
  computation of $\algo{OnSign}$. By (P2), $\adv$ cannot distinguish this
  simulated share from a genuine one. Once $|S(\vec{m})|$ reaches $t - |\Cor|$,
  $\advB$ instead queries its own signing oracle for $\vec{m}$, and uses the
  values exchanged during the (possibly interactive) offline phase, simulated
  as above, to construct a signature share $\sigma_i$ that, combined with the
  shares it has already issued, yields a signature verifying correctly under
  $\TSPScombine$ and $\SPSverify$. In both cases, $\advB$ can respond to every
  query of $\adv$ without ever calling its own signing oracle on a message for
  which it would later need to produce a forgery.

  Since $\advB$ never queries its own signing oracle on $\vec{m}^*$ itself, if
  $\adv$ outputs a forgery $(\vec{m}^*, \signature^*)$ satisfying the winning
  condition of the adp-TS-UF-1 game, then $(\vec{m}^*, \signature^*)$ also
  satisfies the winning condition of the wUF-CMA game for $\advB$. Combined
  with the perfect simulation established above, the advantage of $\adv$ in the
  adp-TS-UF-1 game is therefore bounded by the advantage of $\advB$ in the
  wUF-CMA game of the underlying structure-preserving signature scheme, which
  is negligible by (P1):
  %
  \begin{equation*}
  %
    \Adv_{\TSPS, \adv}^{\text{adp-TS-UF-1}}(\secparam) \leq \Adv_{\mathrm{SPS},
    \advB}^{\text{wUF-CMA}}(\secparam) \leq \operatorname{negl}(\secparam).
  %
  \end{equation*}

  \paragraph{Handling corruption.} It remains to handle $\adv$'s queries to
  $\corrupto$. $\advB$ simulates these directly, returning the sampled $\sk_k$
  for each newly corrupted party, until $|\Cor| = t$. Beyond this point,
  $\advB$ cannot return a further share $\sk_k$ consistent with $\pk$ without
  being able to reconstruct the full secret key itself, and correspondingly the
  adp-TS-UF-1 game's winning condition requires $|\Cor| < t$. For $|\Cor| < t$,
  every query to $\corrupto$ can be simulated as above, so the reduction goes
  through, and the threshold structure-preserving signature scheme achieves
  adaptive TS-UF-1 security with corruption bound $|\Cor| < t$. This concludes
  the proof of~\Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security}.

\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of~\Cref{thm:tsps-security}}\label{sec:tsps-proof-2}

\begin{proof}

  It suffices to check that each of the two schemes of
  \Cref{sec:tsps-instantiation} satisfy the properties
  of~\Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security}.

  For (P1), the scheme of \Cref{sec:tsps-sps-ggm} is existentially unforgeable
  in the generic group model, and the scheme of \Cref{sec:tsps-sps-kiltz} is
  existentially unforgeable under the DDH assumption in both source
  groups~\cite{C:AGHO11,C:KilPanWee15}. Of the three notions of
  \Cref{sec:tsps-definitions}, wUF-CMA is the weakest, since its winning
  condition is the most restrictive: it excludes any $\mssg^*$ the adversary
  queried, whereas UF-CMA and sUF-CMA admit forgeries on queried messages.
  Existential unforgeability of either scheme therefore gives wUF-CMA security.

  For (P2), both threshold schemes share their secret key using the
  $t$-additive sharing of \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc}, which is linearity-preserving
  and private against any set of at most $t$ signers by assumption.

  For (P3), the required decomposition is exhibited for each scheme alongside
  its description: in \Cref{prot:tsps-abe} the message enters only through the
  terms $\mu_i\rho{m}_i$, and in \Cref{prot:tsps-kiltz} only through $(\gen,
  \mssg)\boldsymbol{\kappa}$. In both cases a secret-shared field element
  multiplies a public group element, which by the linearity-preservation of
  \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc} each signer computes locally, and the remaining online
  operations are additions of shares, also local. Everything else in either
  signing algorithm depends only on the secret key and on randomness sampled
  independently of the message, and so is carried out during $\algo{OffSign}$.

  Both schemes therefore fall within the scope
  of~\Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security}, which gives the claim.

\end{proof}
