\section{Construction}\label{sec:tsps-construction}

In brief, the construction proceeds as follows. The signing key of an
underlying structure-preserving signature scheme is secret-shared among $n$
signers. Rather than reconstructing it, the signers use the MPC protocol of
\Cref{sec:tsps-mpc} to jointly compute shares of every value that the signing
algorithm derives from the key and from its own randomness. All of this is done
before the messages to be signed are known. Once a message vector arrives, each
signer performs a purely local computation to produce a signature share, and
any party holding $t+1$ shares combines them into a signature that verifies
exactly as an ordinary signature of the underlying scheme would.

The construction is generic in the underlying structure-preserving signature
scheme $\SPS$, subject to one requirement, which both schemes of
\Cref{sec:tsps-instantiation} satisfy. Write $\SPSsign(\sk, \mssg)$ as a
computation on the secret key, on randomness sampled during signing, and on the
message vector. We require that this computation decomposes into a
message-independent part, consisting of samplings from $\Zp$ and of field and
group operations that $\algo{MPC}$ provides, and a message-dependent part that
uses only operations the signers perform locally: addition of shares, and
multiplication of a secret-shared field element by a public group element.
Since the messages of a structure-preserving signature scheme are themselves
group elements, and are public at signing time, this second condition is what
makes the online phase non-interactive. It is the linearity-preservation
property of \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc}, applied to the message vector, that supplies
it.

We assume throughout that the signers agree in advance on the parameters
$(\ell, t, n)$, and that $\algo{MPC}$ is available to them. How $\algo{MPC}$ is
realised is orthogonal to the construction; \Cref{sec:tsps-evaluation}
discusses two concrete realisations. We now present the construction in full.

\paragraph{Setup.} During setup, the signers fix the vector length $\ell$, the
corruption threshold $t$, and the number of signers $n > t$, together with the
description of the pairing groups over which the underlying
structure-preserving signature scheme is defined.

\paragraph{Key generation.} Key generation produces a public key distributed
exactly as one produced by $\SPSgen$, together with a $t$-additive sharing of
the corresponding secret key. The signers first sample shares of each field
element of the secret key, then lift those shares to the group elements of the
public key, and finally reconstruct the public key alone, keeping the secret
shares.

The signers invoke $\algo{RandShare}$ once for each field element of $\sk$,
obtaining $t$-additive shares $\shr{\sk}$. Where the underlying scheme derives
further secret material from these values, they invoke $\algo{Mul}$, and add
shares locally, to obtain shares of the derived values. Each signer then lifts
its shares to the group elements comprising $\pk$, computing $\shr{X} =
\shr{x}\gen$ and $\shr{\widetilde{X}} = \shr{x}\ggen$ against the appropriate
generators, which by linearity-preservation requires no interaction. Finally
the signers call $\algo{Open}$ in each source group to reconstruct and publish
$\pk$. Each signer stores its share of $\shr{\sk}$, together with any shares of
derived values that the online phase will need. If any call to $\algo{MPC}$
returns $\bot$, the signers abort.

\paragraph{Offline signing.} The offline phase performs, ahead of time, every
part of signing that does not depend on the message. Because $\SPSsign$ samples
its randomness independently of the message, and because the values derived
from that randomness are likewise message-independent, this phase absorbs all
of the interaction that threshold signing requires. It can be run repeatedly to
build up a store of pre-processed material, one entry per future signature.

On input the secret key shares, the signers invoke $\algo{RandShare}$ to obtain
shares of the randomness that $\SPSsign$ samples, and then $\algo{Mul}$,
$\algo{Inv}$, and $\algo{ExpGrp}_1$ as the scheme requires, interleaved with
local liftings to the source groups and local additions of shares, to obtain
shares of every value the signing algorithm derives from the secret key and
that randomness.  Those components of the signature that do not depend on the
message are complete at the end of this phase, and are stored as
$\shr{\signature}_{\algo{OffSign}}$ along with the shares the online phase
consumes. If any call to $\algo{MPC}$ returns $\bot$, the signers abort.

\paragraph{Online signing.} Online signing takes the message vector and
produces a signature share. By the requirement above, the only operations
needed at this point are addition of shares and multiplication of a secret
share by a public group element, both of which each signer performs locally. No
signer communicates with any other during this phase, and its cost is
comparable to that of an ordinary invocation of $\SPSsign$.

On input a message vector $\mssg \in \group{1}^\ell$, each signer combines its
share of the pre-processed material with $\mssg$, multiplying its shares of
field elements by the public group elements of $\mssg$ and adding the results,
to produce its share $\shr{\signature}_{\algo{OnSign}}$ of the
message-dependent part of the signature. Both operations are local by
linearity-preservation, so this phase makes no call to $\algo{MPC}$ at all.

\paragraph{Combining.} Combining reconstructs the signature from the shares. It
is performed by whichever party is to hold the signature, and needs no
participation from the signers beyond their having published their shares.

On input $t+1$ signature shares, the combining party calls $\algo{Open}$ in
each source group to reconstruct each group element of the signature from the
corresponding shares, and returns $\signature$. If any call returns $\bot$, it
returns $\bot$ instead.

\paragraph{Verification.} Verification is that of the underlying
structure-preserving signature scheme, unchanged.

On input $(\pk, \mssg, \signature)$, the verifier returns $\SPSverify(\pk,
\mssg, \signature)$; that is, $\TSPSverify = \SPSverify$.

\paragraph{Robustness and abort.} In the honest-majority setting, where $t <
n/2$, the signature can always be reconstructed from the shares of the honest
signers alone, so the scheme is robust. In the dishonest-majority setting,
where $t > n/2$, robustness is no longer guaranteed. If the realisation of
$\algo{MPC}$ supports identifiable abort, however, then so does the threshold
signature scheme: a corrupt signer that contributes a bogus share causes an
abort in which it is identified, and can be excluded from subsequent signing.
Which of the two settings applies is a property of the realisation, not of the
construction, and \Cref{sec:tsps-evaluation} benchmarks one of each.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:tsps-construction-security} Let $\SPS$ be a
  structure-preserving signature scheme satisfying wUF-CMA security whose
  signing algorithm admits the decomposition described above, and let
  $\algo{MPC}$ satisfy the properties of \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc}. Then the
threshold structure-preserving signature scheme obtained from $\SPS$ by the
construction above is adaptive TS-UF-1 secure, for any corruption bound $|\Cor|
< t$.  \end{theorem}

\Cref{sec:tsps-instantiation} turns this construction into full descriptions of
two threshold structure-preserving signature schemes, and
\Cref{sec:tsps-security} proves both secure.
