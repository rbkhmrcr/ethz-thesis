\section{Construction}\label{sec:tsps-construction}

In brief, the construction proceeds as follows. The signing key of an
underlying structure-preserving signature scheme is secret-shared among $n$
signers. Rather than reconstructing it, the signers use the MPC functionalities
of \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc} to jointly compute shares of every value that the
signing algorithm derives from the key and from its own randomness. All of this
is done before the messages to be signed are known. Once a message vector
arrives, each signer performs a purely local computation to produce a signature
share, and any party holding $t+1$ shares combines them into a signature that
verifies exactly as an ordinary signature of the underlying scheme would.

The construction is generic in the underlying structure-preserving signature
scheme $\SPS$, subject to one requirement, which both schemes of
\Cref{sec:tsps-instantiation} satisfy. Write $\SPSsign(\sk, \mssg)$ as a
computation on the secret key, on randomness sampled during signing, and on the
message vector. We require that this computation decomposes into a
message-independent part, consisting of samplings from $\Zp$ and of field and
group operations that the functionalities of \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc} provide, and a
message-dependent part that uses only the operations those functionalities
perform locally: addition of shares, and multiplication of a secret-shared
field element by a public group element. Since the messages of a
structure-preserving signature scheme are themselves group elements, and are
public at signing time, this second condition is what makes the online phase
non-interactive. It is the linearity-preservation property of
\Cref{sec:tsps-mpc}, applied to the message vector, that supplies it.

We assume throughout that the signers agree in advance on the parameters $(\ell,
t, n)$, and that the functionalities of \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc} are available to
them. How those functionalities are realised is orthogonal to the construction;
\Cref{sec:tsps-evaluation} discusses two concrete realisations. We now present
the construction in full.

\paragraph{Setup.} During setup, the signers fix the vector length $\ell$, the
corruption threshold $t$, and the number of signers $n > t$, together with the
description of the pairing groups over which the underlying structure-preserving
signature scheme is defined. No trusted dealer is required: the signing key is
never held by any party at any point, including at key generation.

\paragraph{Key generation.} Key generation produces a public key distributed
exactly as one produced by $\SPSgen$, together with a $t$-additive sharing of
the corresponding secret key. The signers first sample shares of each field
element of the secret key, then lift those shares to the group elements of the
public key, and finally reconstruct the public key alone, keeping the secret
shares.

The signers invoke $\idfu{\algo{RandShare}}$ once for each field element of
$\sk$, obtaining $t$-additive shares $\shr{\sk}$. Where the underlying scheme
derives further secret material from these values, they invoke
$\idfu{\algo{Mul}}$ and $\idfu{\algo{Add}}$ to obtain shares of the derived
values. They then invoke $\idfu{\algo{ExpGenGrp}_1}$ and
$\idfu{\algo{ExpGenGrp}_2}$ with the appropriate generators to obtain shares of
the group elements comprising $\pk$, and call $\idfu{\algo{CombineGrp}_1}$ and
$\idfu{\algo{CombineGrp}_2}$ to reconstruct and publish $\pk$. Each signer
stores its share of $\shr{\sk}$, together with any shares of derived values that
the online phase will need. If any functionality returns $\bot$, the signers
abort.

\paragraph{Offline signing.} The offline phase performs, ahead of time, every
part of signing that does not depend on the message. Because $\SPSsign$ samples
its randomness independently of the message, and because the values derived
from that randomness are likewise message-independent, this phase absorbs all
of the interaction that threshold signing requires. It can be run repeatedly to
build up a store of pre-processed material, one entry per future signature.

On input the secret key shares, the signers invoke $\idfu{\algo{RandShare}}$ to
obtain shares of the randomness that $\SPSsign$ samples, and then
$\idfu{\algo{Mul}}$, $\idfu{\algo{Inv}}$, $\idfu{\algo{ExpGrp}_1}$,
$\idfu{\algo{ExpGenGrp}_1}$, $\idfu{\algo{ExpGenGrp}_2}$, and
$\idfu{\algo{MulGrp}_1}$ as the scheme requires, to obtain shares of every
value the signing algorithm derives from the secret key and that randomness.
Those components of the signature that do not depend on the message are
complete at the end of this phase, and are stored as
$\shr{\signature}_{\algo{OffSign}}$ along with the shares the online phase
consumes. If any functionality returns $\bot$, the signers abort.

\paragraph{Online signing.} Online signing takes the message vector and
produces a signature share. By the requirement above, the only operations
needed at this point are addition of shares and multiplication of a secret
share by a public group element, both of which each signer performs locally. No
signer communicates with any other during this phase, and its cost is
comparable to that of an ordinary invocation of $\SPSsign$.

On input a message vector $\mssg \in \group{1}^\ell$, each signer combines its
share of the pre-processed material with $\mssg$, using the local operations
that $\idfu{\algo{ExpGenGrp}_1}$ and $\idfu{\algo{MulGrp}_1}$ perform without
interaction, to produce its share $\shr{\signature}_{\algo{OnSign}}$ of the
message-dependent part of the signature.

\paragraph{Combining.} Combining reconstructs the signature from the shares. It
is performed by whichever party is to hold the signature, and needs no
participation from the signers beyond their having published their shares.

On input $t+1$ signature shares, the combining party queries
$\idfu{\algo{CombineGrp}_1}$ and $\idfu{\algo{CombineGrp}_2}$ to reconstruct
each group element of the signature from the corresponding shares, and returns
$\signature$. If any functionality returns $\bot$, it returns $\bot$ instead.

\paragraph{Verification.} Verification is that of the underlying
structure-preserving signature scheme, unchanged. A verifier given $\pk$ needs
to know neither $n$, nor $t$, nor which signers participated.

On input $(\pk, \mssg, \signature)$, the verifier returns
$\SPSverify(\pk, \mssg, \signature)$; that is, $\TSPSverify = \SPSverify$.

\paragraph{Robustness and abort.} In the honest-majority setting, where $t <
n/2$, the signature can always be reconstructed from the shares of the honest
signers alone, so the scheme is robust. In the dishonest-majority setting,
where $t > n/2$, robustness is no longer guaranteed. If the realisation of the
functionalities of \Cref{sec:tsps-mpc} supports identifiable abort, however,
then so does the threshold signature scheme: a corrupt signer that contributes
a bogus share causes an abort in which it is identified, and can be excluded
from subsequent signing. Which of the two settings applies is a property of the
realisation, not of the construction, and \Cref{sec:tsps-evaluation} benchmarks
one of each.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:tsps-construction-security} Let $\SPS$ be a
  structure-preserving signature scheme satisfying wUF-CMA security whose
  signing algorithm admits the decomposition described above, and let the ideal
  functionalities of \Cref{fig:tsps-ideal-func} and
  \Cref{fig:tsps-ideal-func-group} be realised as defined. Then the threshold
  structure-preserving signature scheme obtained from $\SPS$ by the
  construction above is adaptive TS-UF-1 secure in the $\mathcal{F}$-hybrid
  model, for any corruption bound $|\Cor| < t$.  \end{theorem}

\Cref{sec:tsps-instantiation} turns this construction into full descriptions of
two threshold structure-preserving signature schemes, extending the MPC
functionalities of \Cref{sec:tsps-definitions} to secret-shared group elements
along the way, and \Cref{sec:tsps-security} proves both secure.
