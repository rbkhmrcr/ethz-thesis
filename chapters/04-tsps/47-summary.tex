\section{Summary}\label{sec:tsps-summary}

\begin{outline}
  \item thresholdising an SPS scheme mostly needs only general-purpose MPC: key
generation, combining and verification need only the MPC
interfaces; key secrecy and compatibility with ordinary verifiers follow directly
  \item only signing needs its own design and proof; isolating it to one
property, decomposable signing, keeps the security argument to one reduction and
moves all interaction ahead of the message
  \item the remaining online phase is local, costs about as much as ordinary
signing and as partial signing in the earlier non-interactive threshold schemes
\end{outline}

\begin{outline}
  \item applies beyond our two schemes: any signature scheme whose
message-dependent part is public values raised to secret exponents, generic MPC
for the rest, proof reduced to unforgeability of the underlying scheme
  \item includes other SPS schemes; more generally a way to look at threshold
primitives: separate the part depending on the late input and ask whether it can
be local
  \item then interaction moves offline, and systems prepare in quiet periods for
busy ones (payments, transparency logs)
\end{outline}

\begin{outline}
  \item link to the thesis statement: certifiers of a ledger or transparency log
keep their role
  \item authority divided (no one certifies alone), and they certify committed
values (authority without knowledge)
  \item no interaction once the message is known, so dividing authority costs
little when a signature is needed, which is what makes the role practical for an
institution
\end{outline}

\begin{outline}
  \item open: neither MPC protocol is adaptively secure; with one that is, the
reduction loses $\binom{n}{t}$, large for an honest majority; removing it needs
an equivocable sharing or secure erasures
  \item if the underlying SPS scheme is strongly unforgeable, the same reduction
gives the strong threshold analogue
  \item threshold $\KPW$ offline phase could be cheaper by computing the public
parts locally
  \item future work: other SPS schemes, deployment in the ledgers and
transparency logs of the introduction
\end{outline}
