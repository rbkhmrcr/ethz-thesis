\section{Introduction}\label{sec:tsps-introduction}

Structure-preserving signature (SPS) schemes are those in which messages,
public keys and signatures are all group elements. As all operations in the verification are
algebraic, SPS schemes allow for proofs of signatures that are more compact and efficient to produce than
those of general digital signatures. This makes SPS schemes useful across a
range of privacy-preserving applications, including blind signatures,
delegatable anonymous credentials and group
signatures~\cite{AFRICACRYPT:FucVer10,CCS:CamDriDub17,RSA:CriLys19,PoPETS:MSBM23,AC:Groth06,AC:Groth07,C:LibPetYun15}.

In systems with entities that publicly certify state, such as ledger
administrators or transparency log providers, threshold certification shares
the role of signer between several parties.

In privacy-preserving transaction ledgers, users' tokens or accounts are
generally represented as commitments. To spend a token or update an account,
users need a signature showing that its commitment is on the
ledger~\cite{EPRINT:ACDDET19,EPRINT:TBAAGP22,CCS:KiaKohSar22,RSA:PoiSan18,NDSS:SABMD19}.
With an SPS scheme, the administrator can sign the commitments directly and
users can then prove statements about the committed values in zero knowledge.
Proposed designs for central bank digital currencies use ledgers run by
authorities who each endorse transactions before they are
recorded~\cite{EPRINT:ACDDET19,EPRINT:TBAAGP22}. Threshold SPS schemes
would enable the endorsing parties to collectively construct a single signature.

Certificate transparency (CT) and key transparency (KT) logs are append-only,
publicly verifiable records of certificates or keys signed by log providers.
With a threshold SPS scheme, key owners would be able to efficiently prove in
zero knowledge that their keys are in the log without necessarily revealing
which keys are theirs.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We give two threshold SPS schemes
built from existing SPS schemes and secure multiparty computation (MPC)
techniques~\cite{C:AGHO11,C:KilPanWee15,C:DPSZ12,CCS:KelOrsSch16,C:GLOPS21}.
One instantiation has signatures of three group elements, the smallest possible
for SPS in type III groups, and is secure in the GGM~\cite{C:AGHO11}. The other
has larger signatures and relies only on the SXDH
assumption~\cite{C:KilPanWee15}.

We start from a common construction which applies to any SPS scheme with a
property we call decomposable signing. All message-independent computation runs
in an offline (pre-processing) phase, and once the message is known, signing
needs no interaction.

Computation on shared group elements has been implemented for two
parties~\cite{NDSS:GPVRNC23,LC:ADEO21} and for many parties on curves without a
pairing~\cite{ESORICS:DOKSS20}.  We extend the MP-SPDZ library to share group
elements and perform group operations in pairing-friendly groups, including raising
shared group elements to shared exponents, for any number
of parties~\cite{CCS:Keller20}. To our knowledge, this is the first
multiparty implementation of computation on elements of pairing-friendly groups.

\subsection*{Related work}

\paragraph{Threshold SPS schemes.} Threshold SPS schemes have only recently
been considered. One non-interactive construction is restricted to signing
indexed Diffie-Hellman tuples, of the form $(\gen^\msg,
\ggen^\msg)$~\cite{AC:CKPSS23}. Due to the constraints on the structure of
group elements signed, this scheme cannot be used to sign most existing commitments
or public keys directly.

Following our work, a non-interactive threshold SPS scheme was given that signs
arbitrary group elements and is secure under SXDH~\cite{PKC:MMSST24}. It builds
on $\KPW$, and its signatures consist of seven group elements, against three
for threshold $\AGHO$. We benchmark against it in \Cref{sec:tsps-evaluation}.
Threshold SPS schemes are said to have silent setup if signers generate their
keys independently, and a scheme has been constructed to support this, though
it has larger signatures than our schemes and requires a trusted setup~\cite{EPRINT:GGPR25}.
