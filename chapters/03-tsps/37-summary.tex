\section{Summary}\label{sec:tsps-summary}

\begin{outline}
  \item keep this section and the chapter very brief and concise but correct
from the start: building blocks, then the changes, the implementation, then move
on
  \item restate the core idea in two or three sentences, as in the
introduction, then the online cost: about as much as ordinary signing
  \item threshold $\AGHO$ has the smallest possible signatures of any threshold
SPS for general messages, at the price of the GGM
\end{outline}

\begin{outline}
  \item applies beyond our two schemes: any signature scheme whose
message-dependent part is public values raised to shared exponents, generic MPC
for the rest, proof reduced to unforgeability of the underlying scheme
\end{outline}

\begin{outline}
  \item link to the thesis statement: certifiers of a ledger or transparency log
keep their role
  \item authority divided (no one certifies alone); signers know what they
certify, but the signature is on a commitment, so verifying it needs no
knowledge of the value
  \item no interaction once the message is known, so dividing authority costs
little when a signature is needed, which is what makes the role practical for an
institution
\end{outline}

\begin{outline}
  \item open: adaptive security needs an adaptively secure MPC protocol, and
removing the $\binom{n}{t}$ loss needs an equivocable sharing or secure erasures;
the benchmarked protocols are covered only by the static theorem
  \item if the underlying SPS scheme is strongly unforgeable, the same reduction
gives the strong threshold analogue
\end{outline}
