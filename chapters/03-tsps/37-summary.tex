\section{Summary}\label{sec:tsps-summary}

This chapter gives threshold SPS schemes in which signing needs no interaction
once the message is known. We call the property that allows this decomposable
signing.  Our construction applies to any signature scheme with this property,
structure-preserving or not, and its online phase is local and costs about as
much as ordinary signing.

Threshold $\AGHO$ has the smallest possible signatures of any threshold SPS
scheme for general messages and is secure in the GGM. Threshold $\KPW$ has
larger signatures, of seven group elements versus the three of $\AGHO$
signatures, and its security rests on the SXDH assumption.

Our extension of MP-SPDZ provides implementations of the protocols for any
number of parties. The online phase stays below 2~ms up to sixteen parties, and
all interaction is in the offline phase.  Ledger administrators and
transparency log providers can then split certification between several parties
at little cost.

Several questions remain open. We expect strong unforgeability to hold
when the underlying scheme is strongly unforgeable, but do not prove this.
Under adaptive corruption, our reduction loses a factor of $\binom{n}{t}$,
which is exponential in the number of signers for an honest majority. It also
assumes signers erase intermediate shares. Threshold versions of related signature schemes cannot be proven fully adaptively secure without
modifications~\cite{C:CriSte25}. Our proof also idealises key generation.
It is open under which conditions threshold SPS schemes can be proven
adaptively secure.
