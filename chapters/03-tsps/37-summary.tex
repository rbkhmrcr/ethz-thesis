\section{Summary}\label{sec:tsps-summary}

\begin{outline}
  \item keep this section and the chapter very brief and concise but correct
from the start: building blocks, then the changes, the implementation, then move
on
  \item be open that this is mostly an implementation contribution, plus
splitting signing into offline and online phases and proving security still holds
  \item thresholdising an SPS scheme mostly needs only general-purpose MPC: key
generation, combining and verification need only the MPC
interfaces; key secrecy and compatibility with ordinary verifiers follow directly
  \item only signing needs its own design and proof; isolating it to one
property, decomposable signing, keeps the security argument to one reduction and
moves all interaction ahead of the message
  \item the remaining online phase is local, costs about as much as ordinary
signing and as computing a signature share in the earlier non-interactive threshold schemes
\end{outline}

\begin{outline}
  \item applies beyond our two schemes: any signature scheme whose
message-dependent part is public values raised to secret exponents, generic MPC
for the rest, proof reduced to unforgeability of the underlying scheme
  \item includes other SPS schemes; more generally a way to look at threshold
primitives: separate the part depending on the late input and ask whether it can
be local
  \item then interaction moves offline, and systems prepare in quiet periods for
busy ones (payments, transparency logs)
\end{outline}
