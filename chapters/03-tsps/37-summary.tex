\section{Summary}\label{sec:tsps-summary}

This chapter gave threshold SPS schemes in which signing needs no interaction
once the message is known. Any SPS scheme with decomposable signing can be made
threshold this way. All message-independent computations are done under MPC in
an offline phase, and the online phase is local, costing about as much as
ordinary signing. Unforgeability reduces to that of the underlying scheme.
Threshold $\AGHO$ has the smallest possible signatures of any threshold SPS for
general messages, at the price of security in the GGM. Threshold $\KPW$ has
larger signatures, at seven group elements versus the three of $\AGHO$, but
relies on standard assumptions. The approach applies beyond SPS, to any
signature scheme whose message-dependent part is public values raised to shared
exponents.

Certifiers of a ledger or transparency log keep their role, but no single signer
can certify alone, and dividing the role costs little when a signature is
needed.

Two directions remain for future work. The first is strong unforgeability. We
expect the same reduction to give the strong threshold analogue when the
underlying SPS scheme is strongly unforgeable, but do not prove it. The second
is adaptive corruption. Adaptive security needs an adaptively secure MPC
protocol, and our reduction then loses a factor that grows exponentially with
the number of signers for an honest majority. Removing this loss needs an
equivocable sharing or secure erasures. Threshold Schnorr signatures cannot be
proven fully adaptively secure without modifications~\cite{C:CriSte25}, so the
implications of adaptive corruption for threshold schemes are worth making
clearer. The protocols we benchmark are covered only by the static theorem.
