\section{Summary}\label{sec:tsps-summary}

This chapter gave threshold SPS schemes in which signing needs no interaction
once the message is known. We capture this concept through decomposable
signing.  Any signature scheme with it, structure-preserving or not, can become
a threshold scheme whose online phase is local and costs about as much as
ordinary signing.

Threshold $\AGHO$ has the smallest possible signatures of any threshold SPS
scheme for general messages and is secure in the GGM. Threshold $\KPW$ has
larger signatures, of seven group elements versus the three of $\AGHO$
signatures, and its security rests on the SXDH assumption.

Our extension of MP-SPDZ provides implementations of the protocols for any
number of parties. The online phase stays below 3~ms up to eleven parties, and
all interaction is in the offline phase.  For ledgers and transparency logs,
this lets certification be split between several parties at little cost.

Two directions remain for future work. We expect strong unforgeability to hold
when the underlying scheme is strongly unforgeable, but do not prove this.
Under adaptive corruption, our reduction loses a factor of $\binom{n}{t}$,
which is exponential in the number of signers for an honest majority. It also
assumes signers erase intermediate shares. Threshold versions of somewhat
similar signature schemes cannot be proven fully adaptively secure without
modifications~\cite{C:CriSte25}. Our proof also idealises key generation.
Exploring the conditions under which threshold SPS schemes can be proven
adaptively secure is interesting future work.
