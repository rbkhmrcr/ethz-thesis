\section{Security}\label{sec:tsps-security}

We prove security relative to the properties of the SPS scheme and the MPC
protocol stated in the theorems, and not to any concrete MPC protocol. We use
two consequences of the linear $(t+1, n)$ sharing, for sharings of field
elements and of group elements alike. First, any $t$ shares of a value are
uniform and independent of it, so they may be sampled without knowing the value.
Second, given a value $w$ and any $t$ shares of $\shr{w}$, every other share of
$\shr{w}$ is determined and efficiently computable, since reconstruction from any
$t+1$ shares is a fixed linear map, or a fixed product in a group. The second
lets a reduction that does not know the signing key still produce honest
signers' shares of the public key and of signatures, from the public key and
from the signatures it obtains from its own oracle.

\subsection{Proof of \Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security}: security of the construction}\label{sec:tsps-proof-construction}

\begin{proof}

  Let $\adv$ be a PPT adversary against the TS-UF-1 security of
$\TSPS$ applied to $\SPS$. We build $\advB$ playing the EUF-CMA experiment of
\Cref{exp:euf-cma} against $\SPS$. Recall that $\adv$ wins if the following
hold:
  %
  \begin{equation*}
  %
    \SPSverify(\pk, \vec{\msg}^\star, \SPSsig^\star) = 1, \quad |S(\vec{\msg}^\star) \cap
\mathcal{H}| < t + 1 - |\Cor|.
  %
  \end{equation*}

  We first describe the setup. $\advB$ receives $\pk$ from its challenger. On
receiving $(n, t, \Cor)$ from $\adv$, it samples key shares $\{\sk_h\}_{h \in
\Cor}$ uniformly, which by the first consequence are distributed exactly as in a
real execution. For each group element of $\pk$, it computes the shares of the
signers in $\Cor$ from their key shares where that element is a generator raised
to a component of $\sk$, and samples them uniformly otherwise. It samples the
shares of $t - |\Cor|$ further signers uniformly, which by the first consequence
is again their real distribution, and derives every other signer's share from
$\pk$ and these $t$ shares by the second consequence of linear sharing. The key
shares behind the sampled ones are never needed, since no further signer is
corrupted. $\advB$ sends $\pk$, all $n$ shares of it, and $\{\sk_h\}_{h \in
\Cor}$ to $\adv$.

  $\advB$ answers the offline oracle as follows. On $\mathcal{O}_{\algo{Off}}(\mathsf{sid})$ with
$\mathsf{sid}$ fresh, $\advB$ samples the corrupted signers' shares of the
output of every call to $\algo{MPC}$ in $\TSPSoffsign$ uniformly, which by the
first consequence is their real distribution, since each output is a fresh
sharing. It computes their state shares from these and from their key shares,
with the local operations of $\TSPSoffsign$. Where an entry of the state is an
entry of the key, as the $e_{j,i}$ are in $\KPW$, this gives a copy of the key
share. Every value opened in $\TSPSoffsign$ is masked by a fresh uniform value,
so $\advB$ samples these uniformly as well, and runs $\algo{Sim}$ on the
corrupted signers' inputs and outputs and the opened values to produce their
view. If $\adv$ makes the execution abort,
$\advB$ returns $\bot$ and marks $\mathsf{sid}$ unusable. $\advB$ never fixes the
randomness $\varrho$ that $\shr{\state}_{\mathsf{sid}}$ shares. It fixes at most $t$
shares, which are independent of $\varrho$, and $\varrho$ is determined later by
the signature $\advB$ obtains for that session. This is sound because each
session is used for one message only.

  $\advB$ answers the online oracle as follows. Each component of $c_0$ and each
$c_j$ is computed from its own fresh sharing, so any $t$ shares of it are
uniform given the rest of the adversary's view, and so are the signature shares
computed from them. In each session, $\advB$ answers a query to an honest
signer with a uniform signature share as long as the signers of $\Cor$,
together with the honest signers it has answered in that session, number at
most $t$. At the first query that would make this number $t+1$,
$\advB$ queries its own oracle on the session's message, and derives every other
signature share from the signature and the $t$ shares already fixed.

  Finally, $\advB$ forwards $\adv$'s output $(\vec{\msg}^\star,
\SPSsig^\star)$ to its challenger. If $\adv$ wins, fewer than $t + 1 - |\Cor|$
honest signers issued shares on $\vec{\msg}^\star$, so in no session on
$\vec{\msg}^\star$ does $\advB$ query its own oracle. $\advB$ therefore wins
whenever $\adv$ does, and no guess is needed.

\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of \Cref{lem:tsps-adaptive}: security under adaptive corruption}\label{sec:tsps-adaptive}

\begin{proof}

  Let $\adv$ be a PPT adversary against the adp-TS-UF-1 security of $\TSPS$
applied to $\SPS$. $\advB$ is as in the proof of
\Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security}, with the following changes.

  We first describe the setup. $\advB$ receives $\pk$ from its challenger. On receiving
$(n, t, \Cor_0)$ from $\adv$, where $\Cor_0$ is the set of signers corrupted
from the start, it samples a set $\mathcal{K} \subseteq [1,n]$ of
size $t$ uniformly at random, and aborts if $\Cor_0 \not\subseteq \mathcal{K}$.
It samples key shares $\{\sk_h\}_{h \in \mathcal{K}}$ uniformly. $\advB$ does
not know $\sk$, so it can hold at most $t$ shares, and by the first consequence these are
distributed exactly as in a real execution. For each group element of $\pk$, it
computes the shares of the signers in $\mathcal{K}$ from their key shares where
that element is a generator raised to a component of $\sk$, and samples them
uniformly otherwise, which by the first consequence is again their real distribution. It derives
every other signer's share from $\pk$ and these $t$ shares by the second
consequence of linear sharing, and sends $\pk$, all $n$ shares of it, and $\{\sk_h\}_{h
\in \Cor_0}$ to $\adv$. The set $\mathcal{K}$ is a guess at the signers that $\adv$ will
either corrupt or ask for a signature share on its forgery message.

  $\advB$ answers the offline oracle as in the proof of
\Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security}, sampling the state shares of the signers
in $\mathcal{K}$ in place of those in $\Cor$.

  $\advB$ answers the online oracle as follows. On $\mathcal{O}_{\algo{On}}(h, \mathsf{sid},
\vec{\msg})$, if $h \in \mathcal{K}$, then $\advB$ holds $\sk_h$ and
$\state_{\mathsf{sid},h}$, and runs $\TSPSonsign$ itself. Otherwise, the
first time $\mathsf{sid}$ is used, $\advB$ queries its own signing oracle on
$\vec{\msg}$ and obtains $\SPSsig$. It computes the shares
$\{\SPSsig_k\}_{k \in \mathcal{K}}$ by running $\TSPSonsign$ on the material it
holds, derives every other share from $\SPSsig$ and these by the second
consequence of linear sharing, and stores them against $\mathsf{sid}$, so that later
queries in the same session are answered consistently. The share returned is the
unique one consistent with a real execution.

  For the corruption oracle, on $\mathcal{O}_{\algo{Cor}}(k)$, $\advB$
returns $\sk_k$ and every $\state_{\mathsf{sid},k}$ if $k \in \mathcal{K}$, and
aborts otherwise.

  Finally, $\advB$ forwards $\adv$'s output $(\vec{\msg}^\star,
\SPSsig^\star)$ to its challenger. If $\adv$ wins, then $|S(\vec{\msg}^\star) \cap
\mathcal{H}| \leq t - |\Cor|$, so $|\Cor \cup S(\vec{\msg}^\star)| \leq t$, and some
set of size $t$ contains $\Cor \cup S(\vec{\msg}^\star)$. If $\mathcal{K}$ is such a
set, $\advB$ never aborts, and never queries its own oracle on $\vec{\msg}^\star$,
since every signer that issued a share on $\vec{\msg}^\star$ lies in $\mathcal{K}$.
Its output then wins the EUF-CMA experiment. The shares $\advB$ hands out are
distributed identically whichever $\mathcal{K}$ it chose, so $\mathcal{K}$ is
independent of $\adv$'s view, and is such a set with probability at least
$\binom{n}{t}^{-1}$. $\advB$ therefore wins with at least $\binom{n}{t}^{-1}$
times the probability that $\adv$ wins.

\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of \Cref{thm:tsps-security}: correctness and security of the instantiations}\label{sec:tsps-proof-instantiation}

\begin{proof}

  We check that each scheme is EUF-CMA secure and has decomposable signing, and
check the algebra of correctness. We assume the MPC properties.

  $\AGHO$ is existentially unforgeable in the GGM, and $\KPW$ under
SXDH~\cite{C:AGHO11,C:KilPanWee15}. The reduction of
\Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security} only applies group operations to the
group elements it receives, and samples uniform group elements as $\gen^r$, so
it is generic whenever $\adv$ and $\algo{Sim}$ are, and the GGM bound for
$\AGHO$ applies.

  For decomposable signing, the values of $c_0$, the $c_j$ and the $e_{j,i}$
given in \Cref{sec:tsps-instantiation} depend only on the secret key and the
signing randomness. For the two properties of $\TSPSoffsign$ used in the proof,
threshold $\AGHO$ opens only $\gamma = \rho\beta$, masked by the fresh $\beta$.
Its $c_1 = \gen^{\rho}$ is computed from the fresh sharing of $\rho$, and
$\widetilde{r}$ and $\widetilde{s}$ from those of $\beta$ and $\tau\beta$.
Threshold $\KPW$ opens only the values $\epsilon$ and $\delta$ of
$\algo{ExpGrp}_1$, masked by a fresh mixed triple. Each component of $c_0$
other than $\widetilde{q} = \ggen^{\sigma}$, and each $c_j$, contains its own
output of $\algo{ExpGrp}_1$, and $\widetilde{q}$ is computed from the fresh
sharing of $\sigma$.

  For correctness of the threshold $\AGHO$ scheme, the combining party reconstructs
the following:
  %
  \begin{equation*}
  %
    \widetilde{r} = \ggen^{1/\rho}, \quad \widetilde{s} = \ggen^{\tau/\rho} =
\widetilde{r}^{\,\tau}, \quad z = \gen^{\rho} \prod_{i=1}^{\ell}
\msg_i^{-\rho\mu_i} = \left( \gen \prod_{i=1}^{\ell} \msg_i^{-\mu_i}
\right)^{\rho},
  %
  \end{equation*}
  %
  which is $\SPSsign(\sk, \vec{\msg}; \omega)$ for $\AGHO$ with $\omega = 1/\rho$.
For the threshold $\KPW$ scheme, it reconstructs the following:
  %
  \begin{equation*}
  %
    \widetilde{q} = \ggen^{\sigma}, \quad \vec{r} = \vec{b}^{\,\rho}, \quad
\vec{s} = \vec{b}^{\,\rho\sigma}, \quad z_j = \gen^{k_{1,j}}
\prod_{i=1}^{\ell} \msg_i^{k_{i+1,j}} \cdot x_j^{\rho} y_j^{\rho\sigma},
  %
  \end{equation*}
  %
  which is $\SPSsign(\sk, \vec{\msg}; \rho, \sigma)$ for $\KPW$. Both schemes are
therefore instances of $\TSPS$. Correctness follows from correctness of the SPS
scheme and of the MPC protocol, and \Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security} and
\Cref{lem:tsps-adaptive} give security.

  The two threshold schemes follow \Cref{prot:tsps-generic}. Threshold $\AGHO$
computes $1/\rho$ as $\algo{Inv}$ does, with its mask $\beta$ also applied to
$\tau$ to save a round, and stores $c_0 = (\widetilde{r}, \widetilde{s})$,
$c_1 = \gen^{\rho}$ and the $e_{1,i}$. Threshold $\KPW$ stores $c_0$ and the
part $\vec{z}^*$ of each $c_j$ computed with $\algo{ExpGrp}_1$. The rest of
$c_j$, $\gen^{k_{1,j}}$, and the $e_{j,i}$ are computed locally from the key,
so each signer computes them from its key share when signing.

\end{proof}
