\section{Security}\label{sec:tsps-security}

We prove security relative to the properties of the SPS scheme and the MPC
protocol stated in the theorems. As in the experiment, key generation is
idealised, so our proofs do not cover it. The proofs use two facts about linear
$(t+1, n)$ sharing, for sharings of field elements and of group elements alike.
Any $t$ shares of a value are uniform and independent of it, so they may be
sampled without knowing the value. Given a value $w$ and any $t$ shares of
$\shr{w}$, every other share of $\shr{w}$ is determined and efficiently
computable, since reconstruction from any $t+1$ shares is a fixed linear map,
or a fixed product in a group. This lets a reduction that does not know the
signing key still produce honest signers' shares of the public key and of
signatures, from the public key and from the signatures it obtains from its own
oracle.

\subsection{Proof of \Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security}: security of the construction}\label{sec:tsps-proof-construction}

\begin{proof}

  Let $\adv$ be a PPT adversary against the TS-UF-1 security of $\TSPS$
applied to $\SPS$. We build an adversary $\advB$ against the EUF-CMA security
of $\SPS$, as defined in \Cref{exp:euf-cma}.

  $\advB$ receives $\pk$ from its challenger and $(n, t, \Cor)$ from $\adv$. It
samples $\{\sk_h\}_{h \in \Cor}$ uniformly, which is their real distribution,
since any $t$ shares are uniform. From these it computes the corrupted signers'
shares of each group element of $\pk$ that is a generator raised to a component
of $\sk$, and it samples their shares of the others. It samples the shares of
$t - |\Cor|$ further signers uniformly, and derives the remaining shares from
$\pk$. Their key shares are never needed, since no further signer is corrupted.
$\advB$ sends $\pk$, all $n$ shares of it, and $\{\sk_h\}_{h \in \Cor}$ to
$\adv$.

  On $\mathcal{O}_{\algo{Off}}(\mathsf{sid})$ for a fresh $\mathsf{sid}$, $\advB$
samples the corrupted signers' shares of every MPC output in $\TSPSoffsign$
uniformly, which is their real distribution, since each output is a fresh
sharing. It then computes their state shares with the local operations of
$\TSPSoffsign$. Every opened value is masked by a fresh uniform value, so
$\advB$ samples these too, and runs $\simu$ on them to produce the corrupted
signers' view. If $\adv$ makes the execution abort, $\advB$ returns $\bot$ and
marks $\mathsf{sid}$ unusable. As each session signs one message only, the at
most $t$ shares that $\advB$ fixes are independent of the session's signing
randomness, which the signature $\advB$ later obtains determines.

  In the online phase, each component of $c_0$ and each $c_j$ comes from its
own fresh sharing, so any $t$ signature shares are uniform given the rest of
$\adv$'s view. In each session, $\advB$ answers honest signers with uniform
signature shares while they and $\Cor$ number at most $t$. At the query that
would make this $t+1$, $\advB$ queries its own oracle on the session's message,
and derives the remaining shares from the signature and the $t$ shares already
fixed.

  Finally, $\advB$ forwards $\adv$'s output $(\vec{\msg}^\star, \SPSsig^\star)$.
If $\adv$ wins, fewer than $t + 1 - |\Cor|$ honest signers signed
$\vec{\msg}^\star$, so $\advB$ never queried its oracle on $\vec{\msg}^\star$,
and its output is a forgery. The simulation differs from a real execution only
in the $q$ offline sessions, each by at most $\Adv^{\mathsf{mpc}}(\secparam)$,
which gives the bound.

\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of \Cref{thm:tsps-security}: correctness and security of the instantiations}\label{sec:tsps-proof-instantiation}

\begin{proof}

  $\AGHO$ is EUF-CMA secure in the GGM, and $\KPW$ under
SXDH~\cite{C:AGHO11,C:KilPanWee15}. \Cref{sec:tsps-instantiation} shows that
both have decomposable signing and that combining reconstructs an ordinary
signature, so correctness follows from that of the SPS scheme and of the MPC
protocol. The reduction of \Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security} applies only
group operations to the group elements it receives, and samples uniform group
elements as $\gen^r$. So it is generic whenever $\adv$ and $\simu$ are, and the
GGM bound for $\AGHO$ applies. The remaining conditions of
\Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security} hold. Threshold $\AGHO$ opens only
$\gamma = \rho\beta$, masked by the fresh $\beta$, and threshold $\KPW$ opens
only the $\epsilon$ and $\delta$ of $\algo{ExpGrp}_1$, masked by fresh mixed
triples. In both, each component of $c_0$ and each $c_j$ comes from its own
fresh sharing or its own output of $\algo{ExpGrp}_1$.

\end{proof}

\subsection{Adaptive corruption}\label{sec:tsps-adaptive}

In the adaptive variant of TS-UF-1, adp-TS-UF-1, the adversary also has a
corruption oracle~\cite{C:CriKomMal23}. On $\mathcal{O}_{\algo{Cor}}(h)$, the
oracle returns $\perp$ if $h \in \Cor$ or $|\Cor| = t$. Otherwise it moves $h$
from $\mathcal{H}$ to $\Cor$, and returns $\sk_h$ together with every
$\state_{\mathsf{sid},h}$. The winning condition is unchanged, with $\Cor$ the
set at the end of the experiment, and we write
$\Adv_{\TSPS, \adv}^{\mathsf{adp\text{-}TS\text{-}UF\text{-}1}}(\secparam)$
for the advantage. Prior work uses this oracle with the winning condition of
TS-UF-0, and we use it with that of TS-UF-1~\cite{C:CriKomMal23}.

Returning only key and state shares assumes that honest signers erase the
intermediate shares of the offline phase. In threshold $\AGHO$, the signers'
shares of $\rho$, $\beta$ and $\tau\beta$ are among these. The MPC protocol must
also be secure with abort against up to $t$ adaptively corrupted parties.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tsps-adaptive} Under the conditions of
\Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security}, with the MPC protocol secure against up
to $t$ adaptively corrupted parties, for every PPT adversary $\adv$ against the
adp-TS-UF-1 security of $\TSPS$ applied to $\SPS$, there is a PPT adversary
$\advB$ such that the following holds:
  %
  \begin{equation*}
  %
    \Adv_{\TSPS, \adv}^{\mathsf{adp\text{-}TS\text{-}UF\text{-}1}}(\secparam) \leq \binom{n}{t}
\cdot \Adv_{\SPS, \advB}^{\mathsf{euf\text{-}cma}}(\secparam) + q \cdot
\Adv^{\mathsf{mpc}}(\secparam).
  %
  \end{equation*}
  %
\end{lemma}

The loss is the factor $\binom{n}{t}$. With a dishonest majority, $t = n-1$,
this is $n$, linear in the number of signers. With an honest majority, $t
\approx n/2$, it grows exponentially in $n$. If the MPC protocol is secure
against adaptive corruption, threshold $\AGHO$ and threshold $\KPW$ are
adp-TS-UF-1 secure with this loss.

\begin{proof}

  Let $\adv$ be a PPT adversary against the adp-TS-UF-1 security of $\TSPS$
applied to $\SPS$. $\advB$ is as in the proof of
\Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security}, with the following changes.

  $\advB$ receives $\pk$ from its challenger. On receiving
$(n, t, \Cor_0)$ from $\adv$, where $\Cor_0$ is the set of signers corrupted
from the start, it samples a set $\mathcal{K} \subseteq [1,n]$ of
size $t$ uniformly at random, and aborts if $\Cor_0 \not\subseteq \mathcal{K}$.
It samples the key shares and the shares of $\pk$ as in the proof of
\Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security}, with $\mathcal{K}$ in place of $\Cor$
and the further signers, and sends $\pk$, all $n$ shares of it, and $\{\sk_h\}_{h
\in \Cor_0}$ to $\adv$. The set $\mathcal{K}$ is a guess at the signers that $\adv$ will
either corrupt or ask for a signature share on its forgery message.

  $\advB$ answers the offline oracle as in the proof of
\Cref{thm:tsps-construction-security}, sampling the state shares of the signers
in $\mathcal{K}$ in place of those in $\Cor$.

  $\advB$ answers the online oracle as follows. On $\mathcal{O}_{\algo{On}}(h, \mathsf{sid},
\vec{\msg})$, if $h \in \mathcal{K}$, then $\advB$ holds $\sk_h$ and
$\state_{\mathsf{sid},h}$, and runs $\TSPSonsign$ itself. Otherwise, the
first time $\mathsf{sid}$ is used, $\advB$ queries its own signing oracle on
$\vec{\msg}$ and obtains $\SPSsig$. It computes the shares
$\{\SPSsig_{h'}\}_{h' \in \mathcal{K}}$ by running $\TSPSonsign$ on the material it
holds, derives every other share from $\SPSsig$ and these, and stores them against $\mathsf{sid}$, so that later
queries in the same session are answered consistently. The share returned is the
unique one consistent with a real execution.

  For the corruption oracle, on $\mathcal{O}_{\algo{Cor}}(h)$, $\advB$
returns $\sk_h$ and every $\state_{\mathsf{sid},h}$ if $h \in \mathcal{K}$, and
aborts otherwise.

  Finally, $\advB$ forwards $\adv$'s output $(\vec{\msg}^\star,
\SPSsig^\star)$ to its challenger. If $\adv$ wins, then $|S(\vec{\msg}^\star) \cap
\mathcal{H}| \leq t - |\Cor|$, so $|\Cor \cup S(\vec{\msg}^\star)| \leq t$, and some
set of size $t$ contains $\Cor \cup S(\vec{\msg}^\star)$. If $\mathcal{K}$ is such a
set, $\advB$ never aborts, and never queries its own oracle on $\vec{\msg}^\star$,
since every signer that issued a share on $\vec{\msg}^\star$ lies in $\mathcal{K}$.
Its output then wins the EUF-CMA experiment. The shares $\advB$ hands out are
distributed identically whichever $\mathcal{K}$ it chose, so $\mathcal{K}$ is
independent of $\adv$'s view, and is such a set with probability at least
$\binom{n}{t}^{-1}$. So
  %
  \begin{equation*}
  %
    \Adv^{\mathsf{euf\text{-}cma}}_{\SPS,\advB}(\secparam) \geq
\binom{n}{t}^{-1} \left(\Adv^{\mathsf{adp\text{-}TS\text{-}UF\text{-}1}}_{\TSPS,\adv}(\secparam)
- q \cdot \Adv^{\mathsf{mpc}}(\secparam)\right),
  %
  \end{equation*}
  %
  which rearranges to the bound of \Cref{lem:tsps-adaptive}.

\end{proof}
